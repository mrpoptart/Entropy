# Revision Brief — Chapter 12

## Critical Issues (must fix)

**1. Closing inventory paragraph (lines 769-771) — trailing reframe.**
"the city beyond the curve had, in one day, written a law and printed a list and asked a Wellspring to oppose it and a leader to defend it and a worker to choose, and the Wellspring had said no twice and had not said yes once, and the worker had said *both,* and the worker was at her bench, and the Wellspring was at his, and the file was the file, and the gear was the gear, and the tooth came clean." — narrator-side moral framing that grades the day's outcomes, scores Ash's refusals, elevates characters to their thematic functions ("a Wellspring," "a leader," "a worker"). The redeeming feature: it ends on "He filed the next." which is properly literal. But the inventory clause itself is the chapter's most exposed trailing reframe and is also the chapter's most-grading sentence. Cut from "the city beyond the curve" through "the tooth came clean." Keep "Tesha, at her bench, worked the seal seat clean. The lamp by the door burned down a quarter and Sun came in to trim it without speaking and went out." Then: "He filed the next."

**2. Ash's refusal speech to Dorenne (lines 215-223) — composed rhetoric reading as Davan, not Ash.**
"Because if I stand up, and I tell the powerless to be patient, I will spend the rest of my life knowing I did it. Because the only thing I have that nobody else has is twenty-three years of being on the other side of the lattice. And if I use that to defend the lattice as it is, I have, I have nothing left that is mine." — anaphora ("Because... Because... And if..."), parallel structure, clean rhetorical landing. The single "I have, I have" stammer is doing all the work but the architecture around it is too clean. Ash under maximum pressure should produce more chatter-armor, not platform sentences. The narration at line 213 already gives him a beautiful concrete inventory (children at the canal pump, leaning across his sister at eleven, his hand on the rim pretending) — none of it makes it into the dialogue, which reaches for "the powerless" abstractions instead. Both Voice Ash and Voice Dorenne agents flagged this. Revise to break the rhetorical shape: insert hedges, route through one specific concrete image (the basin he leaned across at eleven, or the kid at the canal pump), and let the speech assemble in the moment rather than arrive pre-shaped.

**3. "The mechanism is the lattice, to them..." reply (line 223) — Ash speaking in Dorenne's cadence, unmarked.**
The anaphora on "The mechanism" is Dorenne's institutional resource-allocation rhythm. If this is intentional drift (Ash borrowing her sentence shape to argue against her), the prose must mark it; it doesn't. Either flag the borrowing in narration ("He heard himself reaching for her cadence and could not stop") or de-anaphorize per Critical Issue 2. Voice Dorenne agent confirmed the cross-character collapse from her side; Voice Ash agent confirmed from his. Treat as confirmed cross-character collapse.

**4. Tesha's three-week secret arrives told, not seeded — needs upstream plant.**
The reveal "I've been going for three weeks" lands hard precisely because it's withheld, but Ch10 (the only prior Tesha-heavy chapter) does not show her as politically conflicted. Per the Plot Sync Rule, the fix is to propagate backward: add a small earlier beat in Ch10 or Ch11 where Tesha hesitates around a Leveler topic, asks an unanswered question, or registers a broadsheet differently than the rest of the crew. As written in Ch12 alone, the reveal works dramatically but exposes a missing upstream seed.

**5. "Mira" name collision — cloth-stall woman vs. Stonecurve crew worker.**
Ch10 establishes Mira as a Stonecurve crew worker who appears repeatedly through Ch10 and recurs in Ch12 itself ("Mira's coming with me on the Tuesdays" line 663, "He told Mira. Mira told me" line 741). The cloth-stall woman at line 353 is also called Mira. Different person, but the name reuse will read as confusing — the Stonecurve Mira appears within 300 lines. Rename the cloth-stall woman.

**6. Branner discrepancy between Ch12 and Ch13 outline.**
Ch12 establishes Branner only as a Sixth Ward elder hoarder-list entry whose daughter is married to Pell, and as someone who "warmed our wall, the second winter." The Ch13 outline cast note (per Continuity Tracker) describes Branner as "Building Six pump operator on the closing walk; continuity from Ch12." These appear to conflict. Reconcile in this pass per Plot Sync Rule.

**7. New named infrastructure not in gazetteer.**
Per the Location Name Rule, the following must be added to the Book 2 gazetteer this pass: lower aqueduct lattice, south hospital warming circuit, south escarpment reinforcement, two named bath-houses (or named generically with a function tag), public heating runs of the canal blocks, Sixth Ward, Carver Lane, second ward. Reconcile "lower aqueduct lattice" with Book 1's Kelleren and Thornwall aqueducts (is it the same system, a subsystem, or distinct?).

## Important Suggestions (should address)

**9. Davan's third "Tell me the line" answer to Ash about "urgency vs. building" (lines 695-701) — composed campaign-stump rhythm.**
"Because urgency is louder than building. Because *now* is louder than *next year.* Because a child carrying water tonight is louder than a pump that will work in a winter she may not see." — same triadic anaphora as Issues 2 and 3. **13. Trim or vary the "X was the X" tic — density too high.**

**16. Maren undertone is in the closing thought-list, not on the walk home.**

Tesha herself does not become the wisest person; the narration nominates her. The line is the chapter's thesis sentence, but it delivers the structure the reader has already absorbed from action. Consider trimming or letting the question stand as a question without the architectural summary.

**18. The redistribution ordinance "coordinated draw" mechanism may read as pool-transfer.**

The chapter's framing reads correctly as "channeled service on a schedule" (the pool-debtor model from worldbuilding.md), but the prose phrase "contribute a percentage of their daily pool to a coordinated draw" can also read as pool-donation. One small noun-phrase tightening in Dorenne's exposition (line 165 or 175) would clarify that the contribution is channeled service, not pool donated to a reservoir.

**19. "The lattice runs on the care" (lines 175-178) — slightly closer to "magic responds to emotion" than the system allows.**
