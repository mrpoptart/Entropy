## Best Passages

### Carver

**1. The listening passage (lines 487-491)**

> "Davan did not, in the listening, prepare his next question while Ash was answering. He listened to the answer until the answer ended. Then he was, in the small visible turning of his attention, with the answer for a beat. Then he asked the next thing. The next thing was, in every case, the thing the answer had not yet covered."

Davan's quality of attention rendered as a physical and temporal phenomenon. "The small visible turning of his attention" earns the phrase "the thing the answer had not yet covered." Cited in the revision brief as best-quoted passage.

**2. The file's two registers (lines 121-122)**

> "Haran was, behind the file, thinking of the next thing he was not going to say, and Ash had been in the workshop long enough to know the file's two registers, the file that was filing and the file that was a man's hands keeping busy while a man kept the rest of himself out of the room."

Earns Haran's silence as craft knowledge rather than narrative assertion.

**3. The beech-walnut exchange (lines 463-473)**

> "'Walnut.' / 'Beech.' / 'Mm. I never know.' / He smiled... a small smile, half-formed, the smile of a man who had said *I never know* about something he could have known in two seconds because he wanted, in that two seconds, to be a man who did not know a thing."

The revision brief identifies this as the chapter's best small moment and specifies it should be preserved exactly.

### Hearthkeeper

**1. The prescription confrontation (lines 683-688)**

> "I, I, Davan, I lived in that argument. I read it, in my head, at twenty-one, in every essay in the upper-terrace journals. I hated the man who wrote it. I am sitting at this bench and I am the man who wrote it. / He stopped. / He had said it with his hands closed around the cup and his eyes on the bench, and the saying had been, the way the saying had been on the night he had told his mother about the job, raw, too loud. It had cracked, and the crack had been in the part of his voice he could not, in the year, scaffold over."

The chapter's best character moment across all three drafts. The fracture in Ash's voice rendered physically.

**2. The Haran exit beat (lines 601-609)**

> "Haran poured. He poured for three. He set the cups on the bench... he took, off the peg by the door, the heavy coat he had not worn since the autumn, and he put it on, and he went out by the back door without saying anything, and the back door closed, and Haran was gone. / Davan watched him go."

"The heavy coat he had not worn since the autumn" adds the calendar weight of the gesture.

**3. Haran returning with tea (lines 763-774)**

> "Haran came in with his coat on... He came to the stove and put the kettle on, and the kettle began, on the heat, the small sound it had begun to make when Davan had been at the door, and Haran took it off before it boiled and poured for one cup, and he set the cup on the bench, in front of Ash, beside the cold cup."

Physical-labor-as-care. "Beside the cold cup" is the chapter's most concentrated image.

### Lyricist

**1. The bead-at-the-seal motif (lines 131-136)**

> "'What does the bead tell you,' Davan said... 'When it's hot enough it slips. Right now it's holding. So it's not.'"

Operates on two levels: worldbuilding exchange about pressure and heat, and structural metaphor for where Ash and Davan's relationship stands.

**2. The Davan biography speech (lines 525-542)**

> "I built the movement the way you built the seals: in pieces, with a fuse at every joint, with a pin where the pin would have to go when the seal had to come out."

The workbench analogy for movement-building earns the chapter's central parallel without the author pointing at it.

**3. The "square as vocabulary" observation (lines 161-162)**

> "It was Haran's *mm.* Ash registered the borrowing... The square was, in its small ways, a vocabulary."

Most economical worldbuilding sentence in any draft.

## Weakest Passages

### Carver

**1. The sleep simile (line 913)**
Post-failure trailing reframe, 100% cut applies. Mandated cut to "He did not sleep for a long time."

**2. "It built the sentences I'm using now." (line 711)**
Revision brief Issue 6: witty, self-aware, epigram-complete. Ash at this arc position has not yet processed his arc.

**3. The bedroll reckoning (lines 900-911)**
Ash has worked out the prescription's failure in mathematical terms. If this conclusion is settled in Ch9, his engagement with Davan in subsequent chapters becomes managed knowing dissent. Land it as anxious suspicion, not verified proof.

### Hearthkeeper

**1. "Days passed. / They were the days the days had been before the Cooperage, with one difference" (lines 507-508)**
"The days the days had been" is a tautology dressed as rhythm. Efficient but below the surrounding level.

**2. Dael absent from the tour (Issue 12)**
Outline specifies Dael ("district workers, observed during the tour"). Neither Hearthkeeper nor any other draft includes him. Single named beat resolves this.

**3. "Being angry didn't build anything." (line 359)**
Still arrives as a completed moral sentence. Hearthkeeper earns it better than Carver (with concrete particulars), but a clean revision pass would fracture it further.

### Lyricist

**1. The closing description (lines 780-782)**
"The dark windows of the canal quarter were, he knew without looking, dark." -- redundant. "The square outside the workshop window was the square" -- aestheticizes through tautology.

**2. Davan's biography close (lines 540-548)**
"You build, in the warm. I level, in the warm." -- too neatly symmetrical. Deflates the tension the chapter is building.

**3. The unnamed sixty-one-year-old woman's death (lines 741-755)**
Not present in the outline. Changes Davan's closing question from philosophical to occasioned. Assembly agent must decide deliberately before absorbing.
