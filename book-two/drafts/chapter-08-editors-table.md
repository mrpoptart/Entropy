## Overview

Three drafts cover the same structural requirement: Ash reads the Levelers' platform, attends a rally, hears his own argument weaponized against him, and returns to the proof-of-concept unable to act. The chapter's pivot is Davan's "citizen" line.

Key divergences:
- **Rally location**: Carver = the Cooperage (outline-specified). Hearthkeeper = Foundry Yard (invented -- Location Name Rule violation). Lyricist = Halver's Square.
- **Davan introduction**: Carver keeps him unnamed in the morning transcript. Hearthkeeper names him in the transcript. Lyricist names him fifth of five Council speakers with six minutes of excerpt.

## Best Passages

### Carver

**1. Lines 37-47 (reading the seventh page)**

> "He had been the Wellspring for a year. He had been, in the broadsheets, a man and a story and an emblem and a punchline... He had not, until this morning, been a category in a Council transcript, listed alongside elder channelers and institutional directors as a class of person whose comfort the city was being asked to stop preserving."

Precise social biography, not self-pity. "A class of person whose comfort the city was being asked to stop preserving" is the most precise single sentence for naming what changed.

**2. Lines 75-79 (the wound of recognition)**

> "The wound the paragraphs opened was not the wound of a thing said falsely about him. The wound was the wound of a thing said truly about a person he had been... He had written *charity is what the powerful offer instead of justice.* He had written it in his head walking past Hellen's house, perhaps."

Emotion rendered as exact specific memory. One hedged detail ("Hellen's house, perhaps") makes the memory authentic.

**3. Lines 263-264 (the wall against his back)**

> "Ash felt the wall against his back. / 'Civic experiment,' Davan said, 'is the form generosity takes when generosity has decided not to be redistribution.'"

Physical anchor and speech line in the same moment. Two sentences, no narration.

---

### Hearthkeeper

**1. Lines 440-445 (agrees below the sound)**

> "He agreed. Not with the conclusion. The conclusion he could already feel the shape of, and the shape of the conclusion was a shape he had been working, in the workshop, against. The conclusion was redistribution... He agreed with the diagnosis."

Architecture of Ash's agreement laid out without labeling emotion. "The shape of the conclusion" is a craftsman's thought.

**2. Lines 460-483 (workshop exchange on the prescription)**

> "Because the math doesn't change... Pooling pools doesn't grow them... You haven't built anything. You've leveled the going-down." / "And he's not wrong about the meantime." / "No." / "And I don't know how to answer him." / "No."

Best Haran-and-Ash exchange in the chapter. "You've leveled the going-down" is the best single restatement of Ash's argument in any draft.

**3. Lines 351-356 (the woman at the crank)**

> "She was cranking. The crank was the crank. Three full turns to prime; six to lift; the small pause at the top of the stroke that Vell had taught the morning crew to take... She was doing it the way Reska did the manifold check, which was the way Tesha did the lap-check, which was the way Haran said most things."

Chain of teaching rendered through physical action. "The way Haran said most things" is the chapter's best thematic compression.

### Lyricist

**1. Lines 273-283 (Vell's face)**

> "She turned. / Her eyes met his across the four rows of people between them... Her face did not change. She did not nod... She looked at him for the length of two breaths, and her face was the face of a fifteen-year-old who had spent the last two months turning a crank in a square he had built and was, this morning, standing in a yard listening to a man explain why the crank was not enough."

The dependency problem rendered through a face. Does multiple narrative jobs at once without labeling any of them.

**2. Lines 298-303 (slope and gap)**

> "The argument that we have all been having for as long as my mother has been alive is whether the slope is a fact of nature or a fact of policy. I am here to tell you that the question is wrong. / A beat. / The slope is a fact of nature. The gap is a fact of policy."

Two-sentence crystallization that earns its beat. Operates on three levels: Davan's organizing principle, the sentence Ash wrote at twenty-two, and the proof-of-concept's structural limit.

**3. Lines 596-600 (Haran's correction)**

> "It's not yours because you thought it first, boy. It's everyone's because the city, at the slope it's at, has been thinking it for a generation... The sentence is the sentence. The question's what you do with it."

"The sentence is the sentence" refuses to adjudicate ownership while pushing Ash toward the actual question.

## Weakest Passages

### Carver

**1. Lines 476-488 (the close)**
Verbatim echo of Ch7's close, plus "The pump was warm." Two mandatory cuts.

**2. Lines 327-330 (editorial double)**
"These were the people he had been building for. The platform on the cart was telling them that building was not enough." Does the reader's interpretive work after showing it already.

**3. Lines 282-291 (the architecture paragraph)**
"Each sentence carried a load. Each pause was a joint." -- Dorenne's deployment-framework register in Ash's thought-voice.

### Hearthkeeper

**1. Lines 3-10 (transcript opening)**
"the way he had learned in the first week to dry his hands on the rag by the door because Haran kept the bench dry" -- exposition about a habit, not the habit in action.

**2. Lines 79-143 (week of broadsheets)**
Time-compression section inserts a week between the transcript morning and the rally. Diffuses the chapter's single-arc spine.

**3. Foundry Yard throughout**
Invented location violating the Location Name Rule. Must be corrected to "the Cooperage" everywhere.

### Lyricist

**1. Lines 617-619 (the Maren shelf line)**
"He did not, in the moment, lift the cloth off the half-beat at his sister's cup." -- opaque reference, reads as editing artifact.

**2. Lines 641-645 (the close)**
"the wall held the words for them either way" -- trailing-reframe violation, narrator grading the wall's meaning.

**3. Lines 392-403 (the naming-the-count passage)**
Four consecutive "registered" sentences plus "registered the registering." Too dense. The "registered the registering" construction is the chapter's single most self-conscious sentence.
