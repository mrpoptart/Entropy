# Revision Brief — Chapter 10

## Critical Issues (must fix)

**1. Reservoir number contradicts the outline — Plot Sync violation.**
Draft has Ash at "thirty-eight in the morning, thirty-seven by chapter end" (line 749). The outline's reservoir table specifies Ch10 should land at ~34%, with Ch9 closing at ~36%, and explicitly designates Ch10 as the "accelerated drain — Ash reverting to fixing things himself." The draft's 1-point-over-the-chapter accounting reads as a small drain, not the accelerated one the outline planned. Either bring the numbers down (open ~36%, close ~34%, make the four repairs cost ~2 percentage points) or update outline.md to match — but the two must agree per the Plot Sync Rule.

**2. Internal accounting contradicts itself.**
Line 177: "Half a percent in two weeks." Line 749: "He had been at thirty-eight in the morning. The cistern had cost half. The flue and the wrap and the seal had cost the rest." The first framing says the cumulative drain across two weeks was half a percent. The second says the cistern alone cost half a percent that morning, with the other three repairs costing the remaining half. These cannot both be true. Reconcile.

**3. "He did not, by morning, bolt it." duplicated verbatim from Ch9 close.**
Both chapters end with this exact sentence. The unbolted-door image was Haran's gesture-warning in Ch9 and was load-bearing there. Repeating it as the literal final sentence of Ch10 collapses the motif into authorial loop. Rewrite Ch10's close — keep the unbolted-door image if needed, but use different syntax, or relocate the bolt motif earlier and end on a different image.

**4. Three consecutive chapter closings on the dark-windows / pump-warm pattern (Ch8, Ch9, Ch10) — must be broken.**
- Ch8: "The reservoir sat. / The dark windows of the canal quarter were dark. / The pump was warm."
- Ch9: "The dark windows of the canal quarter were dark. / The pump in the square was warm."
- Ch10: "The dark windows were dark. The pump was warm."
What was a striking diptych the first time has become a ritual close by the third. The juxtaposition is also functioning as narrator-side moral framing (warm-thing-here vs. dark-thing-elsewhere weighing Ash's local success against canal-quarter cost). Per the No Trailing-Reframe Rule's 50% schedule for non-failure beats, three consecutive uses is well over the limit. For Ch10 specifically: cut from line 817 ("The flange would weep...") through line 825 entirely. The chapter has already landed; the closing image cluster (789-825) is doing too much aesthetic labor. End on "He turned on the bedroll." or on a single physical action.

**5. "I built a monument" interior triplet (lines 781-787) — mandatory cut as trailing reframe.**
"He thought: *I built a monument.* / He thought: *I built a monument and called it a school.* / He thought: *the school is the thing I am building now.*" This is the canonical trailing-reframe shape (literal → metaphor → small wisdom) escalated across three beats, immediately following the failure beat of Davan's "she has nothing to be efficient with" / Ash's "no." The italicized triplet is narrator wisdom in Ash's mouth, with the cadence of an epigraph. The follow-up sentence ("He felt the thought the way a man felt the floor of a long fall, which was a feeling that was not the same as a soft landing and was also not the feeling of falling") is a trailing reframe on the trailing reframe. Cut the entire passage 781-787, or keep only the first beat ("*I built a monument.*") with no metaphor extension, no floor-of-a-long-fall gloss. The "floor of a long fall" image also recurs from line 473 — the duplication makes the second instance feel like the narrator reaching for elegy.

**6. Ryn dormancy reaching structural problem — four chapters absent.**
Ryn has been absent since Ch6 (last referenced via Leska's question, offstage). Four chapters dormant in a row puts her at the outer edge of disconnection risk. Ch10 — a chapter explicitly about Ash discovering he built monuments instead of building people — is the perfect place to register her absence in Ash's interior. The bedroll meditation at lines 800-808 enumerates what Ash thinks about (Tova, Reska's bandage, the western pump). Ryn is conspicuously not on the list. Add one sentence acknowledging her among the un-thought-about people. Single sentence is enough to hold the thread until the planned reconnection.

**7. Haran's "You should have built it the way they can fix it." — voice spec violation.**
The most spec-violating Haran line in the chapter. The spec is explicit: "He doesn't tell people what to do" / "Doesn't tell you what to do. Asks one more question and lets the answer arrive on its own." The construction "You should have..." is exactly the construction the spec names as out of voice; it also crosses into "I told you so" territory (also explicitly banned). Ash has just said the thing himself; Haran does not need to say it back to him in corrective grammar. Convert to either a question ("How do they fix it?"), a flat noticing statement, or cut entirely and let "Rough. Obvious. Redundant." carry the work. This was confirmed by both the Voice Editor and Voice Haran agents.

## Important Suggestions (should address)

**8. Two-day eight-sheet redraft is summarized, not shown — wunderkind-adjacent.**
"He had spent two days at the bench before it came." (line 429) — eight ugly sheets, every joint numbered, every part sourceable at the harbor, plus a fifty-gasket purchase. This is the chapter's biggest pacing shortcut. The reader does not watch a single design choice happen. Add one short scene of Ash at the bench making a specific design substitution (the wing nut for the fused seam, with a visible false start), OR have the Firstday meeting reveal that a sheet is wrong, half-drawn, missing a part — leaning into the "third joint Pell had said was hard" the chapter already establishes. The harbor purchase of fifty gaskets is also offstage; even a half-line ("Haran had walked to the harbor on Seventhday") would honor the time.

**9. "I built it too well. / I built it the way I think. / Elegant. Efficient. Optimized." — Ash voice drift / Dorenne register collapse.**
The triplet to Haran is too composed for a man who has not slept. Per Ash's spec, when uncomfortable he talks more, not less; he doesn't deliver clean rhetorical structures. "Elegant. Efficient. Optimized." is also a Dorenne shape (three abstract single-word values in series, no hedge, no concrete object). The chapter doesn't mark it as drift — Haran absorbs it approvingly, which folds Dorenne-register into the moral argument rather than exposing it. Two options: (a) Break Ash's cadence with stammers and rephrasing ("I built it too well. Or, I built it. The way I think, I mean."), and let Haran do the cleaning ("Elegant. Efficient. Optimized."); (b) Keep the line but have Haran register the drift ("You sound like her.").

**10. Haran's four-line lecture (lines 397-411) needs compression.**
Even excluding the "should have" line (Critical Issue 7), the speech runs four declarative beats in a row. Per Haran's spec: "Asks one more question and lets the answer arrive on its own." Combined with the should-have line and the pipe-aphorism that follows, the echo of Ash's triplet becomes the opening of a lecture rather than a single act of mirroring. Compress to two beats with at least one being a question or unfinished construction Ash has to close. Alternative: convert the wire-on-the-former (literally in Haran's hands during this scene) into the analogy — something wound by hand, slowly, that anyone can unwind, vs. something fused that only the original maker can repair. The spec is explicit: "Whatever's in his hands becomes the analogy." The chapter doesn't reach for it.

**11. Davan's "she has nothing to be efficient with" rebuttal — narrator paragraph (lines 679-689) softens the hit.**
The line itself lands correctly. The framing around it ("He said it without sharpness. He said it with the small calm of a man who had spent six years in a great many rooms saying versions of the same sentence") is strong. But the narrator's paragraph immediately after, which explains in detail what Ash hears (the multiplier-as-subject-change accusation), risks doing the work the silence should do. Per the No Trailing-Reframe Rule's post-failure clause, this should end flat. Cut or substantially compress lines 679-689; let "Neither of them said it. They drank tea." carry it.

**12. "I've been watching Haran" speech arrives more articulated than planted.**
The closing math ("If you could close even half of it, in a generation, the math at three percent changes... It changes the slope") is essay-clean for a man working it out aloud for the first time. Two fixes: (a) Add one earlier moment in Ch8 or Ch9 where Ash silently registers Haran doing more with less (ideally with one of the named comparators — Father, Hellen, or Maren — present in memory). (b) Have Ash falter once more in the middle, or stop and restart on the math sentence so it sounds less like prepared text.

**13. The wrap failure (lines 171-181) lacks human witness — risks pattern-fatigue at the third beat of four.**
The pump (with Tova), flue (with Ema), and cistern (with Reska) all have human costs visible on the page. The wrap is the only purely interior failure, with no named tenant, no dialogue, no witness reaction. To make all four land structurally, give the wrap one human beat: a tenant, a passing neighbor, a worker watching from below. The internal "Half a percent. Pump on Tuesday..." accounting is doing thematic work but cannot do witness work.

**14. Davan-Ash bench-work afternoon is so warm it makes the future break feel like a pivot.**
The chapter banks substantial emotional credit (the bread, the pickle, Pell relaxing, Davan as a man who fits at the bench). Currently the only friction visible is the Nessa rebuttal itself; everything before it is harmonious. Add one small note of friction during the bench-work scene — Davan asking a question whose answer Ash slightly evades — to keep the fault line visible underneath the warmth. This protects the eventual Ch13 break from feeling like a pivot rather than an inevitability.

**15. Maren bridge into Ch11.**
The "shelf" has been closed for four chapters. Per the outline, Ch11 is where Maren's plant detonates. A single sentence in Ch10's closing meditation (Maren's hand stuttering at the tea, recalled alongside Reska's bandage and Tova sending the boy) would warm Ch11's opening without violating the "shelf stays closed" discipline of Chs 7-9; recall is not the same as Ash naming it.

**16. Davan-Pell working scene compression — add one dramatized exchange.**
"By the third hour Pell was asking Davan questions. By the fourth Pell was answering Davan's." The summary is doing the work of relationship development. Given how much of this chapter is about the dignity of people learning at human speed, this paragraph could afford one beat of dramatized exchange (a single question, a single answer) before the time-summary takes over.

## Polish Notes (nice to have)

**17. The "X was the X" tautological pattern density is the highest in the book so far.**
"the square at the second hour was the square at the second hour" / "the lane was the lane" / "the shears were the shears" / "the basin was the basin" / "the fact was the fact" / "the answer was the answer" / "The beat was the beat" — and many more. This is a stylistic signature for the book's voice and does real work in Haran/Ash idiom, but Ch10's density is excessive. Thin by roughly a third.

**18. "He did not, in the doing, do Y" pattern dominates — vary cadence.**
"She did not, in the uncrossing, smile" / "He did not, in the seeing, help her" / "He did not, in the laying, say the thing" / "He did not, in the thinking, feel saved" — repetitive sentence structure flag. Vary or thin.

**19. "He had said *mm* the way Haran said *mm.* He had been saying *mm* the way Haran said *mm* since he was fourteen. He registered, this morning, what he was registering with the *mm.*" (line 419) — three near-identical sentence shapes in a row.**
The observation is good (Ash inheriting Haran's reticence is real arc-relevant noticing), but the prosody draws attention to itself. Tighten.

**20. Week-name formatting inconsistency.**
"Threeday" (unhyphenated), "Fourth-day" (hyphenated), "Sixth-day" (hyphenated), "Firstday" (unhyphenated), "Tenday" (unhyphenated), "Fifthday" (unhyphenated). Pick one format. Copy-edit pass.

**21. Season-week count drift.**
Ch9 placed events in "the third week of the season." Ch10 dinner scene (line 583) places light at "the eleventh week of the season." The cadence "Pump on Tuesday, Flue on Threeday" plus the redraft and Davan's Fifthday return adds up to roughly five weeks, not eleven. Either eight more weeks of unshown time elapsed (which is not signaled) or the season-week count drifted. Reconcile.

**22. "Reska's hand was bleeding" beat (lines 235-237) is buried.**
Haran setting the file down, picking it up again, the registered beat: this is the chapter telling the reader the meeting is now necessary. Sits at the tail of a scene break and competes with the immediate transition to the bedroll. Consider letting the beat breathe an extra line, or moving the bedroll-and-call into the meeting scene's opening so the Haran beat is the cistern scene's landing.

**23. "It was holding because I held it." (line 523) — clean chiasmus, slight Ash voice drift.**
The shape is too good for a man who said *Mm* twice two lines earlier. Soften: "It was holding because. Well. Because I was holding it."

**24. "I'm describing a multiplier." (line 667) — let the Dorenne leak be audible.**
Bare-noun abstraction without hedge. Either keep as deliberate Dorenne-register leak (and have Davan or Ash mark it), or hedge: "It's, I think it's a multiplier. Maybe."

**25. "The pieces I'd channeled in. The fused seams. The reground tolerances. The cap on the flue I had set with my hand." (line 515) — Maren-shaped parallel list.**
Four-item parallel list delivered without hedge. Beautifully written but the Maren collapse pattern. Break one with a hedge or self-correction.

**26. "Ashlyn" inner-voice surfacing (line 115).**
Consistent with protagonist file. First time the full name appears in Book Two prose, embedded as an imagined paternal sentence. Confirm intentional. The "called Ash by everyone" canon still holds because this is imagined, not spoken.

**27. Haran missing a dry aside in two Haran-heavy scenes.**
The morning-tea scene and the closing scene contain zero throwaway humor. The spec calls for at least one dry aside per Haran-heavy scene. Combined with the four-line lesson speech, the chapter tilts Haran toward sage and away from mechanic. Seed one dry aside in the morning-tea scene, muttered at the wire while Ash sits with the cup.

**28. Light/dimming motif underused.**
The lamp Ash carries to Haran's elbow (line 791) is a strong silent gesture of stewardship; consider whether that act could carry more of the closing weight than the dark-windows liturgy is currently asked to do.
