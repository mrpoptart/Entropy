# Revision Brief — Chapter 8

## Critical Issues (must fix)

**1. Closing sentences duplicated verbatim from Chapter 7 — must be replaced.**
"The shelf in the back of the room he set things on was, by morning, fuller than it had ever been. / He did not, by morning, lift the cloth." appear at the end of both Ch7 and Ch8 word for word. This collapses the boundary between chapters and undermines both closings. Ch8 needs a distinct close that reflects this chapter's specific landing: Ash has heard his own argument used to demand his pool, agreed with the diagnosis, held an unresolved suspicion about the prescription, counted sixty dark windows, and not acted. The close should be built from those specific elements, not carried from Ch7.

**2. "The pump was warm" — mandatory cut (No Trailing-Reframe Rule, 100%).**
This is a consolation sentence after a failure beat (Ash has not warmed the canal quarter, has not written the letters, has returned to sleep with nothing resolved). "The pump was warm" offers the narrator's small comfort: at least the thing he built works. This is exactly the post-failure softening the rule prohibits. Cut. The chapter must end on "The dark windows of the canal quarter were dark." or on the reservoir sitting. No warmth, no consolation, no counter-image.

**3. "These were the people he had been building for. The platform on the cart was telling them that building was not enough." — cut both sentences.**
This is the narrator stepping forward to do the reader's interpretive work after the earthquake-faces observation. The reader has just watched Ash recognize every person in the yard; they do not need to be told what this means. Both sentences are editorial narration. Cut. Let Ash's hands going tight at his sides carry the beat.

**4. Towpath: "the distinction every powerful person in the history of the city had made when asked to share" — cut the generalization.**
The full sentence: "The distinction was real, and the distinction was, between Halver's Square and the eastern wall of the Cooperage, the distinction every powerful person in the history of the city had made when asked to share." The generalization to "every powerful person in history" is narrator-shaped, essayist register, not Ash's. It tells the reader what the distinction means across history rather than what it means to Ash in his body right now. Keep "The distinction was real." and stop. The following paragraph ("He had read the sentence in his head a hundred times in a hundred essays") does the specific work better.

**5. Towpath: "the way a man thought two things that were both, simultaneously, true" — cut.**
The closing sentence of the two-thoughts paragraph is a wisdom-framing aphorism grading Ash's intellectual maturity. It softens what should be agonizing by framing it as a sophisticated paradox-holder state. The chapter is positioned at Ash's first full encounter with the thing he can't resolve; he should not arrive at equanimity about it within the same scene. Cut the sentence. End on "He started walking again."

**6. "He did not, in the moment, name the question the dark windows were asking. He did not need to." — cut both sentences.**
This is the narrator pointing at subtext and announcing its existence, which destroys the subtext. The dark windows can ask their question without the narrator flagging that a question is being asked. The following paragraph ("The question was the question Davan had built...") follows cleanly without the setup.

## Important Suggestions (should address)

**7. The diagnosis/prescription distinction arrives with too much certainty for Chapter 8's arc position.**
The outline specifies that Ash and Davan are still in genuine near-agreement through Chapter 9, with the break not becoming destructive until later. If Ash already knows with certainty that "the prescription will not work" here, his Chapter 9 intellectual openness to Davan will feel like bad faith. The prescription rejection should land as a discomfort or a suspicion — something he senses but cannot yet fully argue — not as a settled flat fact he states with "I know the way I know the math at three percent" certainty. The specific revision: render "although he knew... that the mechanisms would not, in twenty winters, do what the platform claimed" as a doubt or unease rather than a verified fact. He fears the mechanisms won't work. He can't yet prove it. That is the correct arc position.

**8. "The sentences on the cart were his" over-explains what the preceding biography already showed.**
The paragraph beginning "The sentences on the cart were his. They were better-built than his..." follows three paragraphs of specific biographical detail (the kitchen at twenty-two, the ledger, the kettle, the upper terraces in winter) that have already shown exactly this. The explicit cap sentence weakens what the showing accomplished. Cut "The sentences on the cart were his" and open the paragraph with "They were better-built than his" — the reader already knows whose they are.

**9. "The beam was bearing him" — cut.**
Keep "the beam was beautiful." The following sentence extends the metaphor to make sure the reader understood it, which is the narrator's hand showing. "The beam was beautiful" closes the image; "The beam was bearing him" explains it. Cut the explanation.

**10. The "citizen" pivot needs one beat of Ash's interior at the moment the word lands.**
The outline calls this the chapter's pivot. The draft delivers the crowd's reaction and the woman's "he's right" but steps off Ash's interiority at the precise moment the word lands on him. The towpath later fills the gap, but the moment in the yard deserves one physical anchor — hands, wall, the borrowed coat — before the chapter turns to the woman behind him. Ash should be briefly, specifically present at the word before the outward observation resumes.

**11. The crowd inventory interrupts Davan's speech mid-momentum.**
Vell, Reska's cousin, the boy who quit, and the earthquake faces are currently observed across a 400-word passage during Davan's speech. This pauses the rally for half a page. Consider distributing the crowd inventory before the speech begins: Vell spotted on the approach or at the gate, the others registered during the crowd-settling moment before Davan mounts the cart. The synthesis line (lines 327-330, the faces and the platform's claim) lands harder when the crowd members were established before the speech rather than during it.

**12. Vell's presence in the crowd should amplify the dependency problem thread.**
The outline specifies that seeing district workers in the Leveler crowd should make the proof-of-concept's structural flaw visible: these people were built for, and building wasn't enough. Currently Vell's presence is handled well emotionally but doesn't collide with the dependency thread. One sentence in Ash's observation should connect what she is doing (checking the pamphlet with the same precision she applies to a brass joint) to what that means for the proof-of-concept: she is applying his training to the question of whether what he trained her for is sufficient. The competence he gave her is being pointed back at him.

**13. Ash's "I was a citizen" should arrive messier.**
The iterated "I was a citizen / I was a citizen for twenty-three years" as clean declarative interior monologue has Davan's platform-repetition shape, not Ash's fractured thought-voice. The spec says his thought would arrive sideways. Revise to something like: "He thought: I was. He thought it two or three ways, none of them landing right. He had been a citizen for twenty-three years. That was the fact. He had been one." The mess, not the declaration, is on-spec.

**14. Canal quarter pool-cost should use physical register, not numeric unit.**
"He could do it at the cost of a percent" imports the author's tracking notation into the prose. Elsewhere, reservoir costs are rendered through physical sensation or comparison to felt prior expenditures. Revise to: "He could do it for what it cost him to fuse the cistern roof in the first week — maybe less." That conveys trivial expenditure without breaking the show-don't-tell principle.

**15. "proof of concept" used three times by Ash — Dorenne vocabulary corruption should be marked.**
Ash uses Dorenne's institutional phrase three times in the towpath reflection without any register-catch. The spec says this kind of vocabulary acquisition is corruption-as-warning-sign and should be legible. At the first use, add a beat where Ash hears himself thinking in her phrase: "Proof of concept, he thought. Dorenne's phrase. He had been using it for four months." Subsequent uses can stay as-is; the reader now has the frame.

**16. Ash's departure from the Cooperage needs one physical anchor.**
"He turned, slowly, against the wall, and went out of the yard the way he had come in" is clean but textureless. The borrowed coat, the cap, the gate — one of these should be present in his body as he leaves, consistent with the spec's concrete-return habit at moments of emotional weight.

## Polish Notes (nice to have)

**17. "The shelf was the shelf it had been the night before, and was not" — cut "and was not."**
The narrator is noting that Ash's perception has changed, which is aesthetic labor the prose should not do. End on "He set the transcript on the under-bench shelf, beside the two letters and the broadsheet." Full stop.

**18. "The architecture analysis paragraph" is Dorenne-register in Ash's thought-voice.**
"The platform on the cart was not a stack of grievances. The platform was a structure. Each sentence carried a load. Each pause was a joint. The argument moved from premise to premise the way a load moved from beam to column..." is a structural analyst's metaphor, not a craftsman's. Consider roughening: the recognition should arrive as a craftsman recognizing a joint he has cut before, not as a planner analyzing load-bearing elements. Example: "He recognized it the way you recognized a joint you'd cut before. The sentences fit. They were carrying something." This is Ash's hands, not Dorenne's deployment diagrams.

**19. Haran: "You'll go" narration framing — strip "aimed" language.**
The prose narrates: "It was not a question. Haran did not, in the next moment, look at him to see whether the not-question had landed where Haran had aimed it." The word "aimed" converts Haran's observation into a directive, which violates his spec (he doesn't tell people what to do). Replace the framing narration: "He did not look up. The file moved." Let "You'll go" read as Haran stating a fact about Ash, not giving him an instruction.

**20. Haran: "Big enough" — evaluative, not Haran's register.**
"Lower city. Old barrel yard. Big enough." — the third beat is a value judgment. Haran gives measurements, not editorial assessments. Revise: "Lower city. Old barrel yard. Holds hundreds, they think."

**21. Haran: "So." opener missing from both appearances.**
The spec lists this as his primary verbal tic. The morning exchange could open: "So. You read it," Haran said. The rally instruction cluster should contain at minimum one "So." or "Hm." at a natural entry point.

**22. Haran: No dry aside in the rally instruction scene.**
The spec says expect at least one dry aside per Haran-heavy scene. The rally exchange runs entirely in sage/logistical register. A throwaway one-liner about the Cooperage's acoustics or barrel-yard crowd management, delivered while filing and not followed up, would satisfy the spec without altering the scene's substance.

**23. "Eighth Session" Council transcript — confirm this is a conscious choice.**
The outline frames the equitable resource allocation subhearing as a threshold moment, but "Eighth Session" implies a process underway for some time. If this signals the movement has been gaining institutional ground gradually (correct), confirm and keep. If not, the session number should be reconsidered.

**24. "the woman in the paper-quarter cap" — identify detail may need strengthening if she recurs.**
The outline does not schedule a follow-up for her. If she is purely atmospheric texture, her current detail level is sufficient. If the intent is to return to her, "broad clean hands / paper-quarter cap" is not a durable identifier — she needs one more specific detail.

**25. Numbers: "Three hundred. Perhaps four" — confirm "four" means 400.**
The intended reading is three hundred, perhaps four hundred. The shorthand is clear in context but could be confirmed with "Perhaps 400" for precision.
