## Best Passages

### Carver

**1. Lines 626-682 (the Building Six rebuild scene)**

> "He stood up. He stood up the way a man stood up after kneeling for an hour on stone, slowly... It was, he registered, walking up the cellar stair behind Sera, the hardest thing he had done since the earthquake."

Physical rising lands the cost without naming it. Leaner than Hearthkeeper's version of the same beat.

**2. Lines 493-514 (Haran's speech framing)**

> "'You built it too well,' Haran said. He said it the way a man said a sentence he had been practicing in his head, not because he was performing, but because he had walked the sentence around for two weeks and had set it down a great many times against a great many shapes, and had decided, at last, on the shape it was."

Best framing of the "You built it too well" speech in any draft. Rehabilitates Haran's lecturing tendency by attributing it to patient thought.

**3. Lines 355-385 (the meeting, hand-raise sequence)**

> "He had not been sure, when he framed the question in his head on the catwalk, whether the silence would come. He had hoped, against his sense of the answer, that it would not come. The silence came."

Triadic repetition correctly deployed at emotional peak. Dael's hand for the chimney question (lines 359-386) is the strongest surprise in any meeting scene: a worker already skilled in tin who was never reached because Ash never asked.

### Hearthkeeper

**1. Lines 763-771 (Davan at Building Six cellar, the hinged collar)**

> "'May I,' Davan said. He was looking at the collar. 'Yes.' He opened it. He opened it the way he had picked up the cap at the western pump the first time he had come, which was with both hands, one at the clasp and one at the hinge, slowly... 'Who taught the cast. Reska. Mira and Tervin learned with her. Who taught Reska the cast. Haran. Haran did the cast. Haran taught Reska. Reska did the cast. Mm.'"

The distinction between "Haran did the cast" (wrong) and "Haran taught Reska. Reska did the cast" (correct) is the chapter's thematic statement compressed into three lines. No other draft renders this moment.

**2. Lines 1340-1366 (Haran's closing speech)**

> "'And he is asking you for a thing you cannot give him,' Haran said. 'And you are building a thing that does not, in his sister's winter, warm her flat.' 'No.' 'And both of those are true.' 'Both.' 'Mm.' He picked up the file. He did not file. He held it. 'You will not, in my workshop,' Haran said, 'have the comfort of one of those being false.'"

Best Haran passage in any draft of this chapter. Declarative without being didactic. The revision brief singles this out for verbatim protection.

**3. Lines 460-479 (bakery-wall, the not-doing)**

> "He did not move toward the pump. He stood with one hand on the cold brick and the other in the pocket of his coat and he watched Tova crank... It was, he registered with the small particular pull of a man finding the floor of a long fall, the hardest thing he had done since the earthquake. The earthquake had been a thing he had done with his body. This was a thing he had to not do with his body."

Physical mechanics of the not-doing render character restraint as embodied action. The earthquake extension ("had to not do with his body") is the precise articulation Carver only gestured at.

### Lyricist

**1. Lines 99-107 (Tova's withheld sentence)**

> "She was, in the holding, making a decision about whether to say a thing... the helping would have been the helping a powerful person did when he wanted to spare himself the shape of what was being said. She did not say it. She said: 'Thank you.'"

Best rendering of the Tova beat in any draft. Makes the act of withholding analytically visible without naming the withheld thing and implicates the reader.

**2. Lines 353-365 ("Ashlyn" / father's voice)**

> "He thought it in his father's voice for half a step. *You built it the way you think, Ashlyn.* His father had not said the sentence... he heard it, this morning, with no one's voice attached, and the sentence walked beside him across the square."

Irreplaceable passage absent from other drafts. Surfaces "Ashlyn" for its first Book Two prose appearance.

**3. Lines 325-338 (Ash's speech at the meeting)**

> "They were not, in the looking up, surprised. They were, in the looking up, allowed."

Single best line in any draft of this chapter. Encodes the power dynamic in five words.

## Weakest Passages

### Carver

**1. Lines 495-506 (Haran's "you should have built it" speech)**
Voice spec violation. "Should have" is corrective-parental -- the Father's register, not Haran's. Ash has already said it himself. Mandatory cut per revision brief Critical Issue 7.

**2. Lines 833-843 (Davan's sister, "patient with the candle")**
"Patient with the candle" is ornate and composed, unlike everything else Davan says. Hearthkeeper's laundry-arms detail and Lyricist's cup detail both ground the decline in physical consequence.

**3. Lines 1033-1041 (the final bedroll triplet)**
"He thought *I built a school.*... He thought *and the man cannot.*" -- trailing reframe of a failure beat. 100% cut applies. The chapter already ends correctly on "The pump in Building Six pulled at seventy-five."

### Hearthkeeper

**1. Lines 83-99 (Mira's cellar introduction)**
Three times as long as the characterization needs to be. Front-loaded before Mira does anything. Keep the burns-at-wrist detail and morning-crew motivation; distribute the rest into action.

**2. Lines 904-946 (the redesign montage)**
Summarized rather than dramatized. The Jora bracket ("You built it so you could") at lines 929-935 is the draft's one dramatized redesign moment. The rest is inventory. See revision note 5.

**3. Lines 1380-1398 (the closing paragraph)**
Dark-windows/pump-warm liturgy that the revision brief flags as Critical Issue 4. Mandatory cut. Also a trailing reframe projecting forward in a consolatory register.

### Lyricist

**1. Lines 781-787 (the "I built a monument" triplet)**
Mandatory cut (revision brief Critical Issue 5). Trailing reframe at 100% post-failure threshold, followed by another trailing reframe on the trailing reframe.

**2. Lines 679-689 (narrator decoding the silence)**
Converts subtext into text. "Ash heard, in Davan's *no,* the sentence Davan had not said, which was..." bypasses the reader's inference. Cut to "Neither of them said it. They drank tea."

**3. Lines 395-413 (morning scene absent)**
The Haran-meets-Ash morning scene is entirely absent. Ash goes to the meeting without any prior exchange with Haran. The meeting consequently lacks the foundation of what Ash has already processed.

## Recommended Path

### Primary recommendation: Hearthkeeper as foundation

Strongest draft on every criterion that matters for assembly. Executes all required outline beats, introduces named cast correctly, handles reservoir accounting with internal consistency, and achieves the difficult Davan dinner with the best hinged-collar payoff and Haran's closing exchange.

Critical Issues Hearthkeeper independently solves: no verbatim dark-windows liturgy repeat, no trailing-reframe triplet, "I built it wrong" correctly placed and earned.

Remaining Critical Issues: dark-windows closing (Critical Issue 4) and Haran's "should have" line (Critical Issue 7) -- both sentence-level cuts.

## Revision Notes

Cut lines 1380-1398. Chapter has already landed at notebook entry ("Built for. Not with."). End on "He went up the stair." The unbolted-door line at 1405 is verbatim Ch9 repeat; rewrite to "Below, in the workshop, the file started again." or cut.

**2. (Mandatory) Cut Haran's "You should have built it" (Critical Issue 7)**
Replace "should have" construction with a question or flat noticing statement. The wire in Haran's hands is the analogy. Option: "'You're winding this one by hand,' Haran said. 'Takes longer.'" Remove one of the four declarative beats in the speech.

**3. (Mandatory) Reconcile sister's name to Nessa**
Hearthkeeper uses Vesna; Lyricist uses Nessa; Carver leaves her unnamed. Outline specifies Nessa. Change all instances of Vesna to Nessa. Update outline cast note per Character Name Rule.

**4. (Mandatory) Add Ryn sentence to the bedroll meditation (Critical Issue 6)**

He had not thought about Ryn in the four days and was, in the not-thinking, aware of the not-thinking the way a man was aware of a door he had decided not to open."
