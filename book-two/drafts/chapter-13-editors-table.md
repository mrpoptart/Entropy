## Best Passages

### Carver

**1. Lines 25-49 (Edda's account)**

> "She told it the way a person told a thing she had been telling herself on the walk up, in pieces, not in order, with the pieces she could not yet say going by in the small breaths between the pieces she could."

Double-remove narrative structure works perfectly. The brother who "clapped" and "came back and didn't eat" and kept saying "I clapped" is the chapter's most devastating observation.

**2. Lines 87-93 (three broadsheets)**

> "The body said *the contribution concluded after a sustained channel,* and the word *sustained* was the word the column had chosen, and the choosing had been a choosing."

"The choosing had been a choosing" does the entire editorial-analysis beat in six words.

**3. Lines 374-382 (public laundry)**

> "The young woman channeling in the corner... the way a person channeled who had not yet learned the depth of her own pool, who was still in the part of the work where the work was generous."

Best single characterization of the Levelers' channeling philosophy in any draft.

---

### Hearthkeeper

**1. Lines 120-163 (Olen's account)**

> "She wasn't proud. She wasn't ashamed. She was, she was tired. She was so tired. She had been tired before she sat down, I think."

Deepest characterization of Sera without direct access to her. The wrist-and-open-channel physical detail is the project's clearest rendering of involuntary channeling.

**2. Lines 519-527 (killing line rendered)**

> "He said it quiet. He had not planned to say it. It had come up out of the conversation the way a stone came up out of a river when the water went down, and he had said it before he had decided to, and once he had said it the room held it the way the room had held *toys.*"

Best rendering of the killing line in any draft. The "toys" callback is earned.

**3. Lines 694-695 (manifold scene close)**

> "...and the warm came from the stone and the stone came from the manifold and the manifold came from the contributions and the contributions came from the platform, and Sera had been in the chair on the platform, and Sera had given three times, and the third time she had given on her own, with a thousand people chanting *more,* and the warm in his hand was the warm she had given, and the warm was real, and the cost of the warm was real, and both were real and both were true."

Chain-of-causation pacing enacts the theme structurally.

### Lyricist

**1. Lines 209-255 (Sera's sister at the door)**

> "Don't be sorry at my door, Wellspring. Be sorry where it costs you."
> "She did not say it cruel. She said it the way a woman said a sentence she had been holding in her mouth all morning waiting for a face to say it to."

Sharpest single line of dialogue across all three drafts. The palm-flat-on-closed-door beat (line 257) earns the style guide's physical-sensation standard.

**2. Lines 651-660 (canal lock)**

> "The lock had run on a draw at the upper gate, a small clean coordinated draw two channelers had done at the dawn watch every day for sixty years, and the draw was gone now because the channelers were gone now, both of them, one to age and one to the second winter, and the draw was a pump now."

Worldbuilding through absence. Change rendered through what is no longer there.

**3. Lines 637-647 (Cooperage yard)**

Physical sensation inventory as emotional register: cold post under his palm, ache at the second knuckle, picture of Davan's hands on the desk. Exactly as the style guide specifies.

## Weakest Passages

### Carver

**1. Lines 477-479 ("Both true." italicized)**
"*Both true.* / He did not say it. He did not need to." -- mild trailing-reframe variant. Narrator does work a character could do. Compare Hearthkeeper's Tesha speaking the synthesis.

**2. Lines 391-392 (three proto-arguments)**
"He had an answer that began with *the river, not the swimmer* and dissolved at the comma." -- "the river, not the swimmer" asks the reader to admire it. Style guide warns against prose that feels like the author showing off.

**3. Lines 504-515 (Drift Report visible)**
Writer-side document embedded in chapter file. Strip before assembly.

### Hearthkeeper

**1. Lines 281-282 (trailing reframe)**
"The drinking from a cold cup was, he saw, a habit that began on a day a person could not have told you afterward." -- No Trailing-Reframe Rule violation. Cut after "He drank it anyway."

**2. Lines 270-272 (Haran explanation)**
"That's the thing the lattice does. The lattice makes the warmth and takes the name off it." -- worldbuilding as exposition rather than natural speech.

**3. Lines 848-849 (final sentence)**
"...and the city, between them, was the city." -- trailing aphorism after a list of specifics. Cut at "the gear, in the clamp at the corner of the bench, was getting clean."

### Lyricist

**1. Lines 545-548 (emotion named)**
"What he had under the rib was not anger. It was grief. / He did not name it." -- internally contradictory style guide violation.

**2. Lines 455-459 (killing line misattributed)**
Outline specifies this is Davan's line. Lyricist assigns it to Ash, reversing the scene's power structure entirely.

**3. Lines 10-12 (exposition)**
"The broadsheet said *for visibility* and the broadsheet meant *for the photograph*" -- repeated three times. Explains what the reader should infer.

## Recommended Path

### Primary recommendation: Hearthkeeper as foundation

Closest match to outline's structural requirements. All seven verbatim exchanges present in correct order. Olen eyewitness (replacing outlined Edda) is a superior dramatic choice -- the wrist detail is thematically irreplaceable. The outline should be updated rather than the draft revised to match.

Two primary liabilities: trailing reframe at line 281, "city, between them, was the city" at line 849, and fishmonger scene length. All revision-addressable.

### Passages to pull from other drafts

**From Carver:**
- Lines 1-121 (Edda scene) -- add as chapter open before Hearthkeeper's broadsheet/Olen sequence. Two eyewitness accounts at different emotional scales together are stronger than either alone.
- Lines 373-427 (south block walk) -- replace Hearthkeeper's manifold scene and broadsheet wall. Carver's walk is populated with bodies doing specific things. More precisely meets the outline's "he passes the communal heating grid... it functions, right now, today, for these people."
- Lines 75-78 (Haran's bread) -- "He picked the cup up / He drank from it. The tea was cold. He drank it anyway." Already in Hearthkeeper but the bread beat is superior in Carver.

**From Lyricist:**
- Lines 172-261 (Sera's sister at the door) -- insert between broadsheet reading and walk to find Davan. Revise Sera's age to sixty-two per outline; soften "Where she was a year ago" to align with outline's "not permanently harmed, recovers within days."
- Lines 651-710 (canal lock scene) -- possible replacement for Hearthkeeper's broadsheet wall if south block walk from Carver is not adopted. Choose one or the other.

### Hybrid structure

1. Carver's Edda scene (lines 1-121) as chapter open.
2. Hearthkeeper's broadsheet-reading and Olen sequence.
3. Lyricist's Sera's sister scene (lines 172-261, revised).
4. Hearthkeeper's Davan confrontation.
5. Carver's south block walk (lines 373-427) replacing Hearthkeeper's manifold/broadsheet-wall section.
6. Hearthkeeper's workshop return and close, with trailing reframes cut.
