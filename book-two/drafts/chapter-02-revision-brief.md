# Revision Brief — Book Two, Chapter 2

## Critical Issues (must fix)

### C1. POV breach — entering Haran's interiority
Line: "his hand was steady and his pencil moved with the quickness of a man writing down something he'd been waiting to hear."
"Something he'd been waiting to hear" asserts Haran's interior state as fact. Ash can observe the quick movement; he cannot know what it means with certainty.
**Fix:** Render as Ash's inference: "his pencil moved with a quickness that looked, to Ash, like a man who'd expected to write exactly that." Or simply: "His hand was steady. The pencil moved fast."
*(Voice Editor, Story Architect)*

### C2. "Three hundred" — numeral formatting
"Three hundred people" should be "300 people" per Chicago Manual style (numerals for 11+).
*(Voice Editor)*

## Important Suggestions (should address)

### I1. Ash's opening workshop dialogue is too flat for a moment of genuine awe
"How long?" and the declarative "You've been designing for a world without magic." — both are efficient in a scene where the spec says warmth/rambly mode can briefly return (with Haran, physically grounded). The notebook reveal is a major emotional moment; Ash gets two clipped lines. The chapter's most emotionally charged beat before the street walk needs at least one fragment of the rambly mode: an almost-joke, a self-conscious apology tucked into a question, a reach for physical detail before the feeling lands.
**Fix:** Convert the declarative to an arriving-at realization: "These are, wait." He turned another page. "Haran. Are all of these for, you've been planning for when it stops. Haven't you." Replace "How long?" with something with more chatter-return texture.
*(Voice — Ash)*

### I2. Planning speech reads as Dorenne — unflagged drift
The planning sequence (lines 121–175) has Ash delivering unhedged scope-talk in Dorenne's institutional register: "We put a header tank," "distribution logic," "retrofit the existing buildings." No character or narrator marks this as wrong. The spec says Dorenne-vocabulary absorption should be "audible" — either someone reacts, or the prose marks the shift.
**Specific flags:**
- "We put a header tank at the high point, there. Gravity feeds down..." → add hedging: "A header tank. Up there, if it's load-bearing. Gravity does the rest, maybe."
- "the temporary pipes already show the distribution logic" → "distribution logic" is institutional vocabulary; replace with "show us where the water wants to go"
- "Retrofit the existing buildings" → "Retrofit" is a policy-document word; replace with "rebuild the walls in the existing buildings"
*(Voice — Ash, Pace Keeper)*

### I3. Triple "I know." reads as Maren
Three consecutive clean one-word acknowledgments to serious warnings (Dorenne's approval; residents' trust; reservoir cost) reads as Maren's certainty-trim, not Ash's elaborating deflection. The spec contrasts them specifically: Maren trims to certainty; Ash elaborates because he isn't certain.
**Fix:** Differentiate the first two; keep the third clean.
- Line 167: "Yeah. I figured."
- Line 171: "I know. That's the harder part."
- Line 175: (beat, then) "I know."
*(Voice — Ash)*

### I4. Planning session arrives at a complete plan with no failure
Ash and Haran work through water, heating, insulation, thermal capture, maintenance structure, and reservoir cost in roughly a page and a half — all proposals confirmed, none rejected or revised. Neither has done district-scale planning before. The absence of any dead end or required revision makes the session read as two people confirming what they already knew.
**Fix:** Introduce one proposal that fails or requires significant revision (a pipe gradient wrong for the street topology; a heat-mass material not sourceable at scale; the header-tank building structurally unsuitable). One correction costs two exchanges and makes the plan feel earned.
*(Pace Keeper, Story Architect)*

### I5. Analytical synthesis arrives too complete in the square
Ash connects water delivery, heating, structural reinforcement, and maintenance dependency within ~60 minutes of first visiting the district. Five distinct connections across four systems with no false starts. Thermal capture is correctly flagged as incomplete; the others arrive whole.
**Fix:** Stage two connections as vague in the square (noticed, not yet understood) and let them click into place during the planning session. Let the reader watch the synthesis build, not find it already assembled.
*(Pace Keeper, Convergence Tracker)*

### I6. Haran's trust narration precedes the interactions that earn it
"Haran had been coming here for years. Fixing things. Asking nothing. Building the kind of trust..." is stated before the resident interactions that demonstrate it.
**Fix:** Move the narration to follow the woman-in-window and shutter-man encounters. The observation should arrive as discovery, not instruction.
*(Pace Keeper)*

### I7. Scene 4 (resident encounters) has two low-value interactions
The woman in the window and the shutter man carry similar emotional register as the thin-faced woman's question, diluting it by the time it arrives as the fourth beat.
**Fix:** Cut or collapse the window woman and the shutter man to a single brief sentence. Let the thin-faced woman's "Who saves us from tomorrow?" arrive with full force, not as the fourth variation on the same note.
*(Story Architect)*

### I8. "We're working on that" — unflagged Dorenne "we" moment
Ash answers the woman's question with "We're working on that." The institutional "we" (placing himself inside a planning apparatus for a human moment) is a Dorenne-register marker the spec says should be audible. It reads as seamless competence here.
**Fix:** Either mark it in narration (Ash hears the "we" and doesn't know who he means), or revise: "There's a plan. It's, we're still working it out." — the stutter and trail-off make the register shift visible.
*(Voice — Ash)*

### I9. Closing interior passage over-explains the chapter's theme
"The plan wasn't about him. That was the part that sat differently, that occupied a space in his thinking he hadn't known was empty. The plan was about a district of three hundred people... About making the extraordinary unnecessary." — this summarizes what the images have already shown.
**Fix:** Cut from "That was the part that sat differently..." through "making the extraordinary unnecessary." The "Not yet" closing does more in two syllables. Trust the images (the girl, the wall, the woman's question).
*(Thematic Compass, Story Architect)*

### I10. Thread 12 (Identity) stated rather than dramatized
Ash's identity shift ("the plan isn't about him") arrives as a reflective summary at chapter's end, not as dramatized resistance. The spec says identity work should show friction: the old reflex firing, being caught, being overridden.
**Fix:** Add one moment during the district walk or planning session where the old identity reflex fires visibly — the pull to channel something for the man who challenged him, or the instinct to demonstrate the Wellspring for the thin-faced woman. He catches it and chooses differently. That single dramatized beat does more for Thread 12 than the current summary.
*(Convergence Tracker, Arc Guardian)*

Clean framed wisdom-delivery reads as a lesson. Haran's method is to hand the mechanism, not the conclusion. Consider: "You weren't asking about the notebooks." Then silence. The notebooks are on the bench.
*(Voice — Haran)*

### P7. Seeds/growth motif absent
The conception of the proof-of-concept plan is a natural planting moment. Even one image — something growing toward light in the workshop, a sketch layout with a garden corner — would activate the motif without announcing it.
*(Thematic Compass)*

### P8. The thin-faced woman needs one grounding detail
She delivers the chapter's central question ("Who saves us from tomorrow?") and exits as a thematic delivery mechanism. One detail (her apartment number, the sound of someone inside, something she's carrying) would make her a person.
*(Thematic Compass)*

### P9. After "Once. Build it. Walk away." — no overflow
This is a big moment; Ash closes it off cleanly. The spec says he trails off rather than escalating, and he does the beat before (line 155). But one additional clause after, something self-conscious about what he just said, would mark this as Ash still processing rather than a confident planner landing a conclusion.
*(Voice — Ash)*

### P10. Heating-stone line — redundant final clause
"The heating lattice in the nearest window flickered, a dim, unsteady glow that meant someone inside was channeling warmth into the wall, feeding it from a pool that wasn't deep enough to hold." — "feeding it from a pool that wasn't deep enough to hold" re-explains what the flicker already shows. Cut the final clause.
*(World Keeper)*
