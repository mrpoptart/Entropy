# Revision Brief — Chapter 7

## Critical Issues (must fix)

**1. "Velden" naming conflict — confirmed critical.**
The chapter introduces "Felor Velden, Middle Quarter Maintenance" as a character. Chapters 1, 2, 4, and 5 all use "the Velden pump," "the Velden system," and "the Velden job" as established project/location references — a quality benchmark Haran invokes. A reader who has tracked "the Velden job" as a named prior installation will stop at Felor Velden and expect a connection that is neither confirmed nor denied. Either: (a) change the character's surname to eliminate the collision, (b) make the connection explicit (Felor Velden is the person whose building/street that pump served), or (c) add a deliberate line acknowledging the overlap. As written the ambiguity is passive noise that cannot survive revision.

**2. Trailing reframes at chapter close — mandatory cuts (No Trailing-Reframe Rule).**
The final three sentences after "He slept" are an elegiac narrator coda that grades the entire chapter's outcome and must be cut. Specifically:
- "The reservoir sat. The wall held. The lamp in the workshop below burned, and the file moved across the brass, and somewhere at the far edge of the square an old woman crossed slowly with a covered pot, and the night, in Stonecurve, was the night the district had been having for two months now, the small ordinary night of a place that worked." — "the small ordinary night of a place that worked" is a textbook narrator-side moral summary. The chapter ends on a failure-adjacent beat (Halver's Square, the unlifted cloth, the math Ash won't look at). The 100% cut rule applies.
- "The shelf in the back of the room he set things on was, by morning, fuller than it had ever been." — narrator arithmetic grading Ash's unresolved burdens. Cut.
- "He did not, by morning, lift the cloth." — wisdom aphorism closing an unresolved emotional note. Cut.
The chapter must end at "He slept." or on the thought of the speaker and the woman in the thin coat just before sleep.

**3. Trailing reframe in the Jora scene — mandatory cut (No Trailing-Reframe Rule).**
"He thought it because the scene was beautiful and the scene was working and the scene was, in every visible respect, the thing he had set out to make. He thought it the way a builder thought *the wall holds* on the first morning a wall held." — the narrator is explaining why Ash thinks what he thinks and grading the scene as already-resolved. Cut both sentences. Keep the italicized thoughts ("this is what I'm building," "this is people teaching people") and nothing else.

**4. Trailing reframe at end of Reska scene — mandatory cut (No Trailing-Reframe Rule).**
"She said it the way she said most things, which was the way Tesha said most things, which was the way Haran said most things, which was, Ash registered without naming, beginning to be the way the district said most things." — this announces cultural convergence as narrator pronouncement, removing Ash's blind spot by having him register it, and grades the project's progress. Cut. The pump-naming detail (Greta, Pat the Younger) already does this work without the gloss.

**5. Haran's letter-scene lecture violates his voice spec and collapses into Dorenne's register.**
"You're building a thing the powerful people don't need and the powerless people can't afford to wait for. Both letters are correct. Both letters are about themselves." — three declarative sentences constituting a political-sociological lesson delivered in Dorenne's resource-allocation vocabulary. Both the Haran and Thematic Compass agents confirmed this. Haran does not tell Ash what to think; he routes meaning through mechanism and returns the question. Replace with a workshop analogy: e.g., "Joint gets stress from two directions at once. Doesn't mean either direction is wrong." Then silence. Let Ash carry the conclusion. Retain only "Both letters are about themselves" if needed, but paired with an analogy, not as a stand-alone thesis.

**6. The dependency problem seed is absent from the Jora scene.**
The outline specifies that Ash should watch the Jora teaching scene and "not see it yet" — the workers are transmitting his framework, not building their own, and the reader should feel the shadow even though Ash doesn't name it. The draft presents only warmth and validation. One concrete beat is needed: when the new recruit writes "the Wellspring says" onto her slate for the second time, Ash should register one half-degree of something wrong — not named, not analyzed, but felt as a slight wrongness in an otherwise correct observation. The double-reading must be available on first read, not only in retrospect. Also: the recruit asking why the count is three, and Jora saying she'll ask Ash rather than answering herself, would be the structural version of this fix.

## Important Suggestions (should address)

**7. "Not today" needs to read as physiological failure, not deliberate restraint.**
The spec says Ash's speech fails at crowd scale — chest tightens, sentences contract. "Not today" delivered "without volume" reads as composed silence in the narration ("he had nothing to say that he had finished thinking"). Deliberate philosophical restraint at podium scale is the one place the spec prohibits Ash from sounding clean. Preserve the two words, but reframe them as what emerged from a failed attempt: a swallowed sentence, or the prose noting that his chest did the thing and "not today" was all that came out, not all that he intended.

**8. The phrase recognition at Halver's Square should land inside the scene, not in retrospect.**
Ash recognizes "the man with an ocean, asking the thirsty to wait" as a phrase from his own internal writing at twenty-one. This recognition is handled in the walk-home retrospect (lines 629-633), not in the moment the speaker says it. The gut-punch belongs in the square, at the moment of impact. Move the recognition into the scene: Ash hears the phrase, knows it, and the knowing is separate from and worse than the crowd turning. The walk-home paragraph can still carry the other threads (the letters, Hellen, the broadsheet) but the phrase-as-own-words revelation should be felt live.

**9. Velden scene: Ash's diagnosis speed is borderline wunderkind.**
Ash produces a complete technical solution with firm timelines (six or eight weeks) for a system in a different district he has never visited, after hearing six parameters, within two minutes. He has solved one district's water problem over two months; he has not done this before for other systems. Add two or three questions from Ash about specifics only a site visit could answer, and have him propose the shape of a solution without committing to timelines until Korrin visits. The meeting can still end productively, but the confidence should be proportionate to what Ash actually knows.

**10. "The math doesn't work at three percent" needs more dramatic prominence.**
This is the Act One shadow the convergence map designates as a primary seed, but it arrives as a parenthetical in a stream-of-consciousness list during the walk home, where it competes with six other registrations. Consider placing it in the cellar scene with Haran after the riser repair — the conversation about Building Four's old risers and the question of piecemeal repair is exactly the context where the scaling math would surface. A single brief thought there, immediately set aside, would give the seed gravity without extending the scene.

**11. Haran's letter-scene advice needs question-first structure.**
"Don't write back today. Don't write back tomorrow. Read them again on Sixth-day. Then write." is three commands in sequence — too instructional per the spec ("he doesn't tell you what to do"). Revise to question-first: "What day is today?" / "Third-day." / "Mm. Read them again on Sixth-day." — then let the "because by Sixth-day you'll know what you think" line arrive as response to Ash's "Why Sixth-day." The content can stay; the command structure must go.

**12. Othen aftermath over-explains — trim to one beat.**
After Haran returns from walking Othen to the bakery, the five-exchange recap (Ash listing what Othen didn't ask about, Haran saying "the vanes") re-explains what the scene already showed. Keep only: "What did he come for. / The vanes. / And the man at The Splits whose name he didn't have." Cut the preceding list of negatives ("He didn't ask about you / He didn't ask about the reservoir / He didn't ask about the catalytic"). Alternatively, add a brief self-deprecating Ash trace before the exchange per Voice note 19 below.

**13. The "He said it the way" construction appears eight times — must prune.**
Instances at lines ~67, 121, 209, 273, 319, 607, 703, 869. By the fifth or sixth occurrence the reader anticipates the construction rather than absorbing the content. Keep at most four; the first two (Haran in the cellar, Haran after the repair) are the most load-bearing and should be preserved. Cut or vary the rest.

**14. Ash's public-speaking failure needs a physiological dimension.**
Aligned with Issue 7: the crowd-facing scene is the place the spec's "chest tightens, sentences contract" register should be most visible. The narration during the crowd turn (the taxonomy of hatred types) reads as Ash-in-retrospect rather than Ash-in-body. A physical grounding detail before the cataloguing — cold, stillness, narrowing — would correct the narrative distance mismatch and embed the experience more viscerally.

**15. Second shelf reference (line 839) is redundant — cut.**
The shelf metaphor is used at line 632 (after the letters) and again at line 839 (after the Jora scene). The first instance earns its place; by line 839 it has become narrator announcement. Cut the line 839 instance. The shelf lives at the chapter's end only in Ash's interiority before sleep — not as a separate scene-closing sentence.

## Polish Notes (nice to have)

**16. Numbers convention: "Thirty people. Forty." should be "30 people. 40."**
Chicago style: numerals for 11 and above. Same for "Twelve hundred households" (1,200) and "Fifteen meters" (15).

**17. Ash's "I'm not hungry" — minor voice drift.**
Clean two-word refusal reads as Maren or Haran. Revise to deflection: "Yeah, I'll, I should, there's bread somewhere, yeah."

**18. Ash's "The work matters more than the credit" — direct moral assertion.**
This should carry the trace of hollowness that Haran's second "Mm" signals. Add a seeking-confirmation move: "The work matters more than the credit, though. Right? That's the thing."

**19. Ash's "We'll be here" — unhedged reassurance to Jora.**
Jora's "If we're still here" is a soft alarm. "We'll be here" shuts it down without qualification. The Halver's Square scene, which immediately precedes the Jora scene, should leave Ash less certain, not more. Revise: "I think so. I mean, Haran's not going anywhere."

**20. Ash's Othen aftermath — add self-deprecating trace.**
After Ash lists what Othen didn't ask about, a single self-deprecating aside would match the spec's "notices when he's been irrelevant and makes the joke rather than naming the sting": e.g., "He didn't ask about me at all, which, fair, I was just the tea delivery."

**21. Ash's "It puts us inside Dorenne's tent" — institutional vocabulary.**
"Tent" as political-framing register is unanchored between Dorenne-speak and Ash's concrete-physical vocabulary. Revise to attribute the observation: "It reads like we're inside Dorenne's tent, which, I don't know, maybe that's not the worst thing, or maybe it is."

**22. Haran's "So." opener missing from broadsheet scene.**
The spec's primary Haran verbal tic is absent from this scene's opening. Micro-revision: "So. You read it," Haran said.

**23. Haran's "Best kind" — preserve as dry-aside.**
Despite borderline flag, this lands as wry mechanic's humor rather than evaluation. Preserve with full surrounding beat.

**24. The Wash Hollow description is slightly tangled.**
"Ash did not know whether Wash Hollow was the name of his community or the name of a place near the name of his community" — one clause too many. Trim to: "Ash did not know whether Wash Hollow was the name of his community or simply a place near it."

**25. Halver's Square is a new location — add to Named Locations gazetteer.**
Market square on the second terrace, broken plinth from the earthquake, introduced Chapter 7. Add per Location Name Rule.

**26. Councillor Hellen's "the pools we already have" — terminology slip.**
Pools are biological and generational; they cannot be "maintained and expanded." Revise to "the infrastructure our channelers maintain" or "the systems we have built."

**27. The "old woman with a covered pot" has appeared three times in three chapters (5, 6, 7).**
The motif reads as intentional district texture, but a third consecutive chapter appearance without the motif doing new work risks attracting reader attention to a pattern that may not be intended to pay off. Track consciously. If retained in Ch7, do not use again until at least Ch10.

**28. Italic formatting inconsistency — walk-home sequence.**
Interior thoughts at lines 723-727 ("I should have spoken," "I should not have spoken," "the woman in the coat," etc.) appear without italics. All direct interior monologue should be italicized per the style guide. Confirm and apply.

## Strengths (what's working well)

**Arc Guardian:** The chapter correctly positions Ash: purposeful, humbled, building rather than saving, not yet confronting the dependency problem. Haran's double "Mm" is the best Haran characterization in Book Two so far — "the sound a man made when the apprentice had said the right thing for the wrong reason and the man had decided not to correct him today." The Halver's Square scene handles the pre-Davan encounter exactly as the outline specifies.

**Voice Editor:** The cold-riser opening is near-perfect medium-distance scene-setting. The Velden scene is an exemplary execution of dialogue as characterization — "I'll take eight" and "He had, Ash registered, done all of his looking before he had come in the door" are two of the best lines in the draft so far. The Open Hand and Hellen letters deliver the political argument without narrator adjudication.

**World Keeper:** Magic costs are rendered entirely through implication and Velden's budget question. "My channelers are putting in two morning shifts to hold it and the second shift is the same five people every day" does more worldbuilding work than a paragraph of exposition and does it invisibly.

**Story Architect:** Three arrivals (Velden, Othen, letters) are correctly sequenced to build multiple axes of pressure before Halver's Square as the climax. The chapter earns its position as the moment of external convergence — the proof-of-concept has attracted attention from three simultaneous directions.

**Pace Keeper:** The Haran repair scene is excellent: failure dramatized in real time, Ash subordinate throughout, Haran's self-correction about doing the joint tired said without drama. The Ryn scene pacing is proportionate. The Halver's Square encounter is handled without softening — Ash loses, the chapter does not redeem it.

**Thematic Compass:** The chapter's thematic register is precisely right for Act One close. The plant of the political binary (neither letter is wrong, both are about themselves) is structurally correct. The broadsheet journalist's framing ("a charming exercise in restraint...undertaken by the most powerful channeler of the age in the spirit of a wealthy man who has chosen, for a season, to walk to work") is the chapter's best single sentence.

**Continuity Tracker:** All Ch7 outline seeds are present. "Tell your father two is fine for a riser. Three is for a pump" pays off the Ch6 Rendell hook cleanly. Maren's half-beat is correctly held on the unlifted-cloth shelf. The scaling-math shadow surfaces at the right moment.

**Convergence Tracker:** The naming of district systems (Greta, Old Pat, Pat the Younger) is excellent double-duty seeding. The Othen scene demonstrates the rural connection without over-explaining its thematic significance. The Jora teaching scene is structurally correct as a convergence between Thread 4 (District) and the dependency seed, pending the shadow-beat fix (Issue 6).
