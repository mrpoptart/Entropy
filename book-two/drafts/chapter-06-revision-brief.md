# Revision Brief — Chapter 6

## Critical Issues (must fix)

**1. Maren is described as Ash's older sister — she is the younger sibling.**
The chapter repeatedly identifies Maren as "the older sister" (lines ~243, ~681). All source files establish her as approximately two years *younger* than Ash. The sibling dynamic — including her guilt about having a pool when he didn't — is built on her watching him struggle from below as the younger one. Every "older sister" reference must be corrected. The "twenty-six years" figure (used twice) should become twenty-four or twenty-five depending on Ash's precise current age. Similarly, the "eight years" since she became a channeler-for-hire and moved out doesn't compute if she left at seventeen; a more consistent figure would be three to five years.

**2. Trailing reframe: workshop window sentence — mandatory cut.**
"Ash sat on the crate by the door and watched the lamp until the workshop window, from outside, would have looked the way it had looked from the lower stair, lit, steady, a small warmth in the dusk holding what it had been built to hold." The final clause ("a small warmth in the dusk holding what it had been built to hold") is the narrator elegizing the infrastructure project as emotional symbol. Cut from "steady" to end of sentence, or cut the sentence entirely. End on the Haran exchange plus Ash going upstairs.

**3. Trailing reframe: shelf clause — mandatory cut.**
"...and the shelf, when he set it down, was fuller than it had been that morning." The shelf accumulation has already been shown. This clause is narrator arithmetic grading Ash's moral state. Cut it. The sentence should end: "He set the question on the same shelf where he had set the half-beat at the cup."

**4. Trailing reframe after Maren's reservoir conversation — mandatory cut.**
"She said it because she loved him and because she could see he was tired and because she wanted him to keep going. She said it and he heard it as a gift and he took it as a gift, and he did not, for the second time in an hour, look closer." The entire first sentence is narrator-side motivation-explaining. "For the second time in an hour" counts instances for the reader. Cut both sentences. Let "Haran's file moved across the bench. Even strokes." close the beat directly.

**5. Second instance of "The reservoir sat. The wall held. He did not, that evening, reach for either." — cut or rework.**
The first instance (line ~419) earns its place closing the family-dinner section. The second, near chapter's end, is a wisdom-sentence grading Ash's discipline. If the reservoir must appear at the close, render it physically ("He lay down") without narrator-side summary of what he did not do.

**6. Reservoir math inconsistency: "thirty months" doesn't compute.**
Ash tells Maren "at three percent a month, about thirty months of work to take the reservoir to the wall." At 3% per month from approximately 39% remaining, the ceiling is roughly thirteen to fourteen months, not thirty. Either the per-month figure is wrong (the outline gives 1–2% per week, i.e., 4–8% per month, which would be even lower), or the starting percentage is wrong. The number needs to be reconciled with the reservoir tracking table in the outline. If "thirty months" is intended, the monthly draw rate must be approximately 1.3%, and the narrative should signal what efficiency gains have been achieved.

**7. Maren's "older sister" farewell lines are in Ryn's register — confirmed cross-character collapse.**
"It's good. Ash. It's good work. I want you to know I see that." and "You'll get it down. You'll find a way. You always have." are both confirmed collapses. Both voice agents (Ash and Maren) flagged the same pattern. Maren elaborates praise to its ceiling of "Good." She does not name her interior ("I want you to know I see that" directly names her emotional intention, which the spec prohibits). "You always have" repeated twice has Ryn's animated warmth, not Maren's compressed certainty. Revise to: "It's good work." then the hug, letting the hug be the rest of the sentence. If the writer wants one more beat: "You'll find the efficiency." (fact-redirect rather than affirmation). The collar-fix gesture, which the spec designates as Maren's signature for "I love you," is absent from this farewell scene entirely; the hug alone can carry it if the thinness of her shoulders is in the narration.

**8. Ash's "I'm fine" is Maren's canonical register — confirmed cross-character collapse.**
The spec explicitly names "I'm fine" as Maren's register and uses it as the definitional contrast ("Where Maren says 'I'm fine,' Ash says 'I'm okay, I'm doing good work, I'm helping people, sorry, what was the question?'"). Both voice agents flagged this line. Revise to something like: "I'm okay, I'm eating fine, there's stew at Haran's most nights, it's not a problem."

## Important Suggestions (should address)

**9. Leska's dialogue is systematically in the wrong mode throughout.**
The spec establishes Leska as cumulative-forensic: continuous narration threading forensic content through her working body, naming what she sees rather than asking diagnostic questions, using her intermediary network openly. Instead the chapter gives her compressed declaratives throughout, which reads as Father's register or Maren's register depending on the line. Specific lines to revise:
- "Tell me about the district." (diagnostic question = Maren): revise to a catalogue that names her information network, e.g., "I hear things. Your father reads what he can. The neighbors know your name before you tell me."
- "That's not telling me." (placed challenge = Father or Maren): revise to narrate the gap, e.g., "I know when you're telling me and when you're saying words." Or: Leska lifts her spoon and says nothing.
- "Slow how." (diagnostic = Maren): revise to statement-from-network, e.g., "Your father says a new hand takes twice as long the first weeks."
- "Did you see Ryn." (diagnostic = Maren): revise to narrate the intermediary chain, e.g., "Ryn sent word through Pera. I didn't know what to tell her."
- "Eat. Eat first. Talk after." (three-period staccato = Father): revise to continuous narration over her working body, e.g., "I'm setting the bowl down. Eat while it's warm. The talking keeps."
- The arrival moment (line ~43) is missing the continuous-narration mode entirely. Leska's forensic surveillance of Ash's thinness should narrate over her body's work.

**10. Maren's two pool signals don't amplify each other — cross-thread gap.**
The pump fatigue and the tea-cup hesitation are each well-executed but are filed by Ash independently with the same verdict both times ("she's working too hard"). The sum should be more alarming than either piece. Add a single beat between the tea-cup scene and the "tell me about scaling" conversation where Ash's awareness of both things flickers into a momentary combined picture before he consciously files it away. This is what makes "the other drawer" feel genuinely threatened, rather than just full.

**11. Haran's "she channels easy" needs grounding in observable behavior.**
Haran has spent the chapter with his back to the room during the tea scene. The reader cannot reconstruct what evidence he drew on. Either give him one unremarked look during the tea scene (head briefly lifting, file stilling), or have Ash register that Haran had been watching when he thought he wasn't. This turns Haran's observation from an authorial intrusion into his characteristic attention.

**12. The closing coda is too long after Haran's "she channels easy."**
The chapter's emotional peak is Haran's exchange with Ash about Maren. Everything after it (Ash-Rendell relay, lamp-watching, square ambiance, sleep) diffuses the impact. Consolidate: keep the Rendell relay ("I keep four"), cut the lamp-watching paragraph, move the Vell's-mother thought directly before sleep, cut the dusk-square ambient paragraph. The chapter ends at approximately "the shelf is fuller than it was" (with the final clause cut per Critical Issue 3) and "He slept."

**13. Father's "you don't engineer for" passage runs too long — crosses into lecture territory.**
The spec says Father drops an analogy and stops; unpacking it across four consecutive sentences reads as lecturing. "That's a thing you don't engineer for. You engineer for it not happening, and then it happens, and then you have to decide whether to count it. / Count it how. / In the design. In what the thing's for. You build a pump because people need water. You don't build it because a girl decides it's hers. But she did. So now what's the pump for." should be trimmed to two or three sentences ending before the question. The question "So now what's the pump for" is also Haran-register (forward-pointing resource question), not Father-register (backward-pointing named act). Cut the question. Let "But she did." close the beat. Ash's insight about "both" can emerge from silence.

**14. "You've still got the wrist" is a general compliment, not a named-act compliment.**
The spec requires Father's praise to be tied to a specific past act ("You cut that drain run clean the year you helped me at Tomek's"), not a general quality. Either convert to a named-act compliment referencing a specific job, or cut the verbal compliment entirely and let Father's gesture (sighting along the pipe, setting it next to the first) be the praise.

**15. Ash's compression is uniformly applied in scenes where warmth should recover.**
"It's working" (line 93), "Good." (line 273), "Thanks." (line 698) are all one-word or two-word answers in domestic scenes where the spec says warmth briefly returns around family and Maren. The chatter-armor pattern is correctly absent from Dorenne scenes; it should be more visible here. At minimum, vary "It's working" and "Good." to add one hedging detail each, and expand "Thanks" to a deflection via Haran ("Yeah. I mean. Haran did most of it, really. Yeah. Thanks.").

**16. The "you'll find a way" beat needs Ash's acceptance of comfort connected to the physical evidence he's been accumulating.**
Maren's reassurance arrives after he has seen her pump fatigue, thin wrist, and tea-cup hesitation. The reader has accumulated more evidence than Ash is acknowledging. One sentence connecting his acceptance of her reassurance to a specific physical image from the day (her wrist at the pump, the coat on the thin hanger) would let the reader feel what he is choosing not to add up.

**17. Ash's "I'll go" / "I will" exchange about Ryn is too compressed for a high-discomfort beat.**
The spec says uncomfortable conversations produce more words from Ash, not fewer. He's doing the same avoidance he did at sixteen, the narration confirms this, but his response matches his mother's compression exactly. Add one over-reassuring clause before the silence falls harder.
