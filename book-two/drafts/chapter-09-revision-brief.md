# Revision Brief — Chapter 9

## Critical Issues (must fix)

**1. Final line "The pump in the square was warm" — duplicated from Ch8, mandatory cut.**
This is the same closing image as Ch8's "The pump was warm." The chapter should close on Haran's unbolted door (the outline's designated gesture-warning) or on the dark windows. "The pump in the square was warm" imports Ch8's consolatory ending into Ch9 and constitutes a trailing reframe after an unresolved beat. Cut. The chapter ends at "He did not, by morning, say what he was not saying." (line 923) or at the workshop-inventory paragraph immediately before it.

**2. Sleep simile (line 913) — mandatory cut (No Trailing-Reframe Rule, 100%).**
"When he did, he slept the way a man slept who had, in the day, met someone he had been waiting to meet, and had found the meeting was not the relief he had imagined, and was, instead, the small clean opening of a door at the end of a hall he had been walking down for a year without noticing the hall was a hall." — this is a post-failure trailing reframe. Ash did not have an answer for Davan's closing question; that is the failure beat. The sleep simile elegizes it as a quiet revelation rather than allowing it to remain unresolved. Mandatory cut. The line before it, "He did not sleep for a long time," is the correct close for that passage.

**3. "He thought it together, the two thoughts, the way a man thought two things that were both, simultaneously, true." — duplicated verbatim from Ch8, must be rewritten.**
This exact sentence appears in Ch8 (line ~427, Ash on the towpath after the rally) and again in Ch9 (line ~909, in the bedroll reckoning). The Ch9 version must be rewritten. The tension Ash holds after this conversation is more specific and more personal than the towpath version — he now knows Davan, has agreed point-for-point, and has no good answer for the dying-in-the-present question. The two thoughts should be rendered as what they specifically are for Ash tonight, not as an abstract paradox.

**4. The bedroll reckoning arrives at too settled an analytical conclusion — violates arc position.**
"He thought because the pump is the answer I built, and the pool is the answer Davan built, and I cannot, this evening, give him the pool because giving him the pool is giving him the prescription, and the prescription will not work, and the not-working is the math at three percent in twenty winters." — this is Ash at Ch8 analytical certainty. The plan positions Ch9 as the "near-total agreement, intellectual chemistry" chapter where the fault line is present but the near-alliance is still real. If Ash has already worked out that the prescription fails (the math at three percent in twenty winters), his engagement with Davan in subsequent chapters collapses from genuine exploration to managed knowing dissent. The prescription's failure should be an anxious suspicion or a discomfort Ash senses but hasn't fully argued, not a settled conclusion he has verified.

**5. "I was angry. For twenty-three years. Being angry didn't build anything." — spec violation and cross-character collapse (Ash/Davan). Confirmed by both voice agents.**
This is a direct emotional naming (the spec's what-he-doesn't-say list prohibits this) rendered as a clean moral aphorism. Davan's immediately preceding line is "I learned, somewhere, that anger doesn't build things" — Ash's version is structurally and morally identical, Davan's conviction imported into Ash's mouth. Ash at this arc position reaches for concrete detail or trails off when emotion goes heavy; he does not issue finished sentences about his own condition. Revise to break the clean rhetorical form: e.g., "I was angry. A long time." then a pause, then "It didn't." (incomplete, trailing). Or route it through a concrete physical detail.

**6. "It built the sentences I'm using now." — most spec-violating line in the chapter.**
This line is witty, constructed, retrospectively self-aware, and has a literary finish that belongs to Davan's rhetorical register, not Ash's. It reads like a man who has already processed his arc and can declare it cleanly. Ash at Book Two mid-point has not processed it. The line should carry the weight of something still live and unresolved, not a delivered epigram. Revise: "I don't know what it built" and a physical action; or "It's still here, I suppose" trailed off; or let the silence be the answer after Davan's question. Any form that breaks the literary-aphorism quality is an improvement.

## Important Suggestions (should address)

**7. "The comfort was over." (line 659) — trailing-reframe sentence, cut.**
This is a small wisdom-sentence glossing Ash's experience of saying "it is not enough" aloud. The context already carries the weight; the narrator's summary ("The comfort was over") does the reader's work for them. Cut.

**8. "It was the first time he had said it aloud." (line 653) — marks significance rather than trusting the reader, cut.**
The cost-language sentences that follow already mark the moment as significant. This notation is redundant. Cut.

**9. Narrator exposition of the pause (lines 681-685) — cut or drastically reduce.**
"Ash heard, in his own sentence, the shape of the thing he was telling Davan, which was that anger was, in his life, a diagnosis and not a treatment..." — this explains what the following dialogue exchange already dramatizes (Davan's counter-questions, the anger exchange). The narrator is translating subtext that immediately becomes text. Cut this paragraph and let Davan's spoken responses carry the weight alone.

**10. Diagnosis scene arrives without friction — needs one small resistance.**
The "naming the rooms" passage has Ash and Davan agree point-for-point without a single misalignment. The reader needs to see the gap before they see the bridge. Add one moment where Ash misreads Davan's framing, or where Davan uses a word (e.g., "redistribution") that makes Ash go still for a beat, before they find the shared observation underneath. One paragraph of near-miss, resolved, earns the arrival.

**11. Davan's backstory is too cleanly purposeful — add one non-functional detail.**
Every element of his disclosed history serves his argument. Real backstory told in conversation has one detail that doesn't cleanly fit the logic — something Davan mentions that doesn't lead anywhere, or a memory that surfaces sideways. One sentence of this type would make Davan feel found rather than constructed.

**12. Tesha and Dael are missing from the tour — Character Name Rule.**
The outline's Ch9 cast list includes Tesha and Dael as "observed during the tour." Both are established named characters from prior chapters. At least one named reference to each during the building tour would satisfy the Character Name Rule. A brief "Tesha was at the riser wrapping on the second floor" would serve.

**13. Vell's "both of them knew where the other had been" framing needs clarification.**
In Ch8, Ash watches Vell from the back of the Cooperage crowd; she never looks back and cannot know he was there. The current framing ("both of them knew where the other had been on Second-day at the seventh hour") implies mutual knowledge. Either add a line explaining how Vell learned Ash was present (someone told her, or she noticed after leaving), or soften to asymmetric knowledge: he knows she was there; she knows he knows; she does not necessarily know he was in the room.

**14. The bedroll five "He thought" lines can be compressed to three.**
The passage from "He thought *I could warm them*" to the end of the interior reckoning runs slightly long. The essential movement is: impulse to warm them / reason he has not / the people in the dark windows need warmth this winter. The analytical middle portion (the prescription-failure math) can be shortened once Issue 4 above is addressed.

**15. "Liss's question was larger than the ledgers" setup exceeds its delivery.**
The narration establishes Liss's question as "larger than the ledgers" (line 731) before she returns. She then asks only about the insulation difference between north and south buildings, which is a small operational question. Either give her a second, weightier observation to justify the setup, or cut "that was, on the way back, larger than the ledgers" and let the scene deliver what it delivers.

**16. The "anger built sentences" exchange needs one beat of Haran reaction.**
"It built the sentences I'm using now" (or whatever replaces it) is the chapter's most resonant moment. Haran is eight feet away winding wire. He hears it. The chapter gives him no reaction — not even a micro-gesture. Haran's presence during the two-hour conversation is justified throughout, but at the chapter's most resonant beat his absence from the scene is a missed amplification. Add one small physical beat: a quarter-inch pause in the winding, a stilling of the hands, something that marks Haran receiving the sentence before the winding resumes.

**17. Korel's canal-quarter flat scene deserves a half-beat more.**
"Ash heard Korel's voice answer, low. He heard Korel laugh, once, at something the woman said. The laugh was the laugh of a man who laughed when something was actually funny." — this is the chapter's warmest moment and it earns more than one sentence. Not a full scene, just one more beat: the woman's voice mentioning her cousin in the canal quarter, and Korel's "tell me about your cousin." The moment is important for the Levelers' moral credibility; an extra half-beat earns it more.

**18. Ash's arc position: the prescription rejection should be suspicion, not certainty.**
Aligns with Issue 4 above but is worth stating separately as an arc concern. The plan's Act Two arc requires Ash to have a stretch of genuine near-alliance with Davan before the break. If Ch9 ends with the prescription's failure as a settled analytical fact, the near-alliance period is compressed before it begins. See Issue 4 for the specific fix.

## Polish Notes (nice to have)

**19. "Apprentice elder" as a term — confirm or replace with canonical vocabulary.**
Davan uses "an apprentice elder" (someone training at full elder capacity). If there is a canonical term for a young elder-class channeler, use it here. If "apprentice elder" is being coined, note it in the glossary.

**20. Western pump vs. east square pump — naming inconsistency.**
Prior chapters (Ch5, Ch7) identify Vell as the operator of the "east square pump." Ch9 refers to it as "the western pump" (line 207). Confirm the correct location name and reconcile across chapters.

**21. "Registering" and its variants appear approximately 40-45 times — prune 15-20 instances.**
The word is the chapter's deliberate stylistic signature and earns its place in many instances. In the long bench conversation section, it clusters to three or four per paragraph. A light pass cutting the weaker instances (especially where the verb adds nothing the context hasn't already established) would sharpen the remaining ones.

**22. Tea offer appears four times in nearly identical form — vary or cut two instances.**
"Tea's hot." / "Tea," / "There's tea," / "Tea." appearing at even spacing across the chapter turns Haran's hospitality move from a characterizing beat into a running joke he doesn't participate in. Keep the first (line 185, where Liss reads the not-offer correctly) and the one at line 435 (after Davan returns alone). Cut or vary the other two.

**23. "He listens." — consider cutting from Haran's closing exchange.**
The outline specifies Haran speaks once ("So.") and otherwise watches. "He listens" arrives as a narrator-adjacent observation from Haran's mouth rather than a resource observation or mechanism analogy. Consider cutting it; keeping only "He's right about the boys" makes the silence around the next thing louder.

**24. "A small cold turn in the chest" (line 155) — minor trailing-reframe adjacent.**
"Which was, he registered with a small cold turn in the chest, an unkindness" — the physical metaphor aestheticizes a cognitive moment. Consider: "which was, he registered, an unkindness, and he set it aside and looked at her." The cold-turn is surplus.

**25. "I never know" / the beech-walnut exchange — preserve exactly as written.**
This is the chapter's best small moment. Do not alter.
