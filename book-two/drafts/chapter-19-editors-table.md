## Best Passages

### Carver

**1. Lines 687-699 (Davan exchange and exit)**

> "This isn't what I built," he said.
> "No," Ash said. "It's what they built."
> Davan did not, for a count, answer. He looked at the basin. He looked at the basin a count. He nodded once, the small short nod of a man who had received a thing he did not want to receive, and he turned, and he walked away. He walked down the slope past the heating row, past the new annex at the back of Building Four, past the door of Tesha's workshop where the lamp was lit at the bench and the door was open. He did not, at Tesha's door, stop. He did not, at the door, look in.

The verbal exchange verbatim per outline. The physical exit delivers the emotional meaning: Davan walking past the open door of Tesha's workshop, where the work he could not join is continuing, and not looking in. The withholding is absolute. The repetition of "He walked... He did not" performs Davan's rigid self-control without naming it.

**2. Lines 725-729 (Ash's reflection at the upper terrace)**

> It was the thing the man had built the ground for. It was not the thing the man had built. The man had built a movement that had won council seats and passed a contribution statute and warmed people who had been cold, and the movement had done the thing the movement had said it would do, and the doing had not been the thing, and the man at the corner of the square had been honest enough to come down across the lower terrace and through the gate and up the lane to look at the thing the doing had not been, and proud enough to walk away from it without going through Tesha's door.

Complex analytical-emotional work in Ash's characteristic register. "Honest enough to come down... and proud enough to walk away without going through Tesha's door" is a precise two-part characterization of Davan that renders admiration without naming it.

**3. Lines 860-870 (Ash answers "You all right")**

> He thought about the walk down from the south block with the inside pocket warm against his ribs and the outside pocket empty... He thought about the small heavy steady thing he had felt at the upper terrace.
> "No," he said.
> "Mm."
> "I'm not."

The chapter's most important emotional disclosure, earned by pages of physical and observational accumulation. The rhythm of the thought-list followed by two brief lines enacts the emotional drop precisely.

### Hearthkeeper

**1. Lines 161-188 (the hybrid heating post, painted seam)**

> The post was a hybrid. Ash recognized the housing from twenty paces. The lower casing was the cast Leveler model, the one with the contribution port and the small embossed mark of the contribution office, and the upper casing was the mechanical backup Tesha had begun fitting to the south block grid in October... Somebody, recently, had painted the seam between the two casings in a flat rust-red, the way a person painted a seam they did not want to pretend was not there.

The best single invented image in any of the three drafts. The merger of two approaches visible in a material object, the join not hidden. "The way a person painted a seam they did not want to pretend was not there" is an entire philosophy in a subordinate clause.

**2. Lines 336-347 (Ash lying on the bed, the clock passage)**

> He listened to the small ticking of Haran's clock downstairs, the one that Haran had, this morning, not given up on, and that was, this evening, running again, in the slow uneven way of a clock that had been talked back into running by a man who knew it well enough to know when to stop turning the screw.

The Haran clock as chapter-spanning structural image (opened at line 7, paid off here). "A man who knew it well enough to know when to stop turning the screw" extends to Haran's character and, by implication, to how Ash has been operating all chapter. Earned.

**3. Lines 263-270 (Haran's "that was a kind thing")**

> "That was a kind thing," he said.
> "Was it."
> "It was. It was also true. The two are not always the same. This evening, on this thing, they were."

Haran's voice held cleanly. The structure of the observation, acknowledging the two things are usually separate and then conceding they coincided, comes from a man who has been thinking carefully for a long time. Rendered in Haran's rhythm, not as aphorism.

### Lyricist

**1. Lines 83-90 (Vanya introduction)**

> Her voice, when it came, was lower than he had expected from a woman of her build, and the rhythm of it was the rhythm of a person who had spent a great deal of time talking to rooms with twenty people in them, which was a rhythm with a small carrying weight at the end of each sentence, the weight of a person used to making sure the back of the room had heard.

Best character introduction in any of the three drafts. Voice rendered through physical and social mechanism rather than adjectives. Tells us Vanya's history and present manner simultaneously.

**2. Lines 139-147 (Vanya's speech about the hall)**

> "The grid is where it is. It's down a third on the spring. It'll be down a third again by the spring after this one. The math is the math. The hall, though, is a hall. The hall is what we have when the grid is gone. The people in the hall are the people."

Supporting character with a distinct rhythm that is neither Ash's nor Haran's. "The people in the hall are the people" lands flat and final. Vanya is audible as a person.

**3. Lines 446-452 (end-of-day passage descending from the south block)**

> He passed Tesha's workshop. The lamp was lit. The door was open. The bench inside had four people at it, and Tesha was at the head, and Vanya was at her left, and Renik was at the bench with the valve, and the man whose stove had not caught at noon a month ago was at the end of the bench with a heating element in his hands, learning. Tesha was speaking. Vanya was listening. The two streams were, at the bench, the same stream.

The merger in action, rendered visually. "Tesha was speaking. Vanya was listening." shows the thing before naming it.

## Weakest Passages

### Carver

**1. Lines 707-717 (closing inventory at the upper terrace)**
"The lane was the lane, and the heating row ran, and the communal plate sat warm at the end of the lane..." The "X was the X" mannerism deployed too many times in one chapter has stopped being expressive containment and become a verbal tic. Physical inventory replacing interiority at the moment the draft needs interiority most.

**2. Lines 898-921 (trailing-reframe close)**
"...a child had had her hand on a warm plate, and a woman from the lower quarter had her hands on a fitting at a bench... and a man in a dark coat the color of wet stone had walked out through a gate without looking back." Narrator-assembled synthesis after the chapter's correct emotional close ("No, I'm not all right"). The three parallel clauses undo the flatness with aesthetic assembly. This is a trailing-reframe violation in its most complete form.

**3. Line 113 ("The page was the page it was")**
Part of a pattern across the chapter: "the bench was the bench," "the workshop was the workshop," "the lane was the lane" appear five or more times. Each instance is defensible in isolation; together they become a stylistic fingerprint that flattens the prose's emotional differentiation. Reduce to two uses maximum per chapter.

### Hearthkeeper

**1. Lines 203-208 (Ash watching Davan walk away)**
"...he went on across the square in the direction he had been going, which was, he realized halfway down the next lane, no direction at all." "No direction at all" names the emotional state rather than showing it. Trailing reframe. Cut at "he went on across the square."

**2. Lines 195-212 (river walk retrospective)**
Three "He thought about Davan in the spring... in the summer... in front of the post" anaphoras. Summary retrospective turning Davan into an abstraction at the moment he should remain concrete. The style guide explicitly names repetitive sentence structure as a rhythmic failure.

**3. Lines 325-348 (bed passage with named resolution)**
"The success was the square. The loss was Davan walking around the post. The two had happened on the same afternoon... and they were not the same thing." Names the success and the loss and separates them for the reader. The chapter has correctly created productive ambiguity at the embankment scene; the bed passage resolves it explicitly, undoing the earlier work.

### Lyricist

**1. Lines 282-312 (Davan broadsheet column section with retrospective summary)**
"A man who knew his arithmetic" repeated three times becomes a verbal trick. The flashback to the hall meeting is the clearest exposition dump in any of the three drafts. Prior chapters have established the Ash-Davan relationship; this summary is not necessary.

**2. Lines 394-432 (basin-cutting scene overruns)**
After the Davan encounter (the chapter's emotional peak), the chapter continues approximately 1,500 words through the basin cutting. The stone-strike anaphora is rhythmically competent but pads after the chapter's natural close. Structural pacing failure: the peak arrives too early in the final movement.

**3. Lines 500-523 (long closing sentence)**
A single periodic construction assembling the chapter's meaning as warmth. "The names in the column would be, when Ash read it, names he had been writing in the book." Does everything the No Trailing-Reframe Rule prohibits. Cut in its entirety. End at "He drank" or at the short Haran exchange ("Mm. Mm.").

## Recommended Path

### Primary recommendation: Carver as foundation

Most structurally faithful to the outline. Staging the Davan encounter at the south block basin (rather than a street market post or a cross-lane corner) keeps Davan in contact with the specific object that represents Ash's work: the basin Liesel installed, the line that came in at the side, the bracket Pell set. This makes "This isn't what I built / No, it's what they built" maximally concrete. Carver also handles the emotional close most correctly: "No, I'm not all right" carries the chapter rather than a narrator summary.

Weaknesses are mechanical and stylistic, not structural: the trailing-reframe close and the over-deployed "X was the X" formulation. Correctible in revision.

### Passages to pull from other drafts

**From Hearthkeeper, lines 161-188 (the painted-seam image)**
Too good to leave in Hearthkeeper only. Transplant to Carver as a detail Ash notices in the south block before seeing Davan: one of the hybrid posts along the heating row, the seam painted rust-red. Three sentences maximum. Insert between Carver's gate arrival and the first Davan sighting.

**From Hearthkeeper: the supper-news structure (adapted)**
Replace Carver's closing workshop section (lines 803-921) with a modified version of Hearthkeeper's supper-news exchange, compressed and adapted to the workshop setting. Include: the Wells Lane vote and the Founders' Hall collapse as political fragmentation parallel. Include the "You all right / No / Mm" exchange from Carver as the emotional close. End there. Delete both the Hearthkeeper bed passage and the Carver trailing-reframe close.

**From Lyricist: Vanya as the chapter's new named character**
Replace Carver's Liesel with Lyricist's Vanya. Vanya has a demonstrably distinct voice profile (carrying-weight rhythm, "The grid'll go. The hall won't." line structure) and the better character-revealing Ash response. The Carver draft retains its Tesha-teaching scene as the structural vehicle but with Vanya substituted and Lyricist's key lines imported.

**From Lyricist, lines 169-182 ("So when the thing works, I know whose idea it was")**
Best line Ash delivers to a new character in any draft. Import as his response to Vanya's question about why he writes names down.

### Hybrid structure

1. Morning broadsheets section: Carver. Reduce "X was the X" to two uses maximum.
2. Renat's people at the south block: Carver. Replace Liesel with Vanya; import Lyricist's voice profile and "know whose idea it was" line.

5. Tesha at the door: Carver as-is.
6. Return to workshop and Haran: Replace Carver's close (lines 803-921) with modified Hearthkeeper supper-news structure, ending on "No, I'm not all right" / "Mm."

## Revision Notes

**1. (Mandatory) Cut the trailing-reframe close (Carver lines 898-921)**
"That was the thing" must be cut. Per CLAUDE.md, the chapter ends on literal action -- the forge ticking, the workshop, the inside pocket warm. The three parallel closing clauses (child with warm plate / woman at bench / man in dark coat) are narrator-assembled synthesis. Delete.

**2. (Mandatory) Reduce "X was the X" formulations to two maximum**
"The lane was the lane," "the workshop was the workshop," "the page was the page it was" appear five or more times. Reserve the mannerism for the workshop opening and the chapter's close. All intermediate instances should be cut or rewritten.

**3. (Mandatory) Replace Liesel with Vanya and import Lyricist's Vanya voice profile**
Vanya has the more distinct voice and the better Ash-response line. Import: the carrying-weight voice description, "The grid'll go. The hall won't.", the contribution-hall list-with-skills-column, and Ash's "So when the thing works, I know whose idea it was."

**4. (Important) Add Hearthkeeper's painted-seam image**
The best single material-world image in any draft. Insert as a detail Ash observes on the heating row before Davan sighting. No more than three sentences.

**5. (Important) Cut Davan reflection trailing-reframe (Carver lines 725-733 vicinity)**
The clause beginning "What he felt instead was the small heavy steady thing..." names and describes the emotion rather than rendering it through physical action. Cut or replace with what Ash does with his hands, what he looks at, what he does not do.

**6. (Important) Verify Vanya's addition to outline cast note**
If Vanya is imported from Lyricist to replace Liesel, add her name and role to the Ch19 outline character list per the Character Name Rule. Reconcile Liesel's outline entry if Liesel is removed.
