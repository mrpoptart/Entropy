## Best Passages

### Carver

**1. Lines 47-66 (the kettle scene)**

> She got up. [...] She took the kettle off the stove and went to the sink and ran water into it and came back and set it on the burner. Then she stood at the stove and reached for the small metal striker on the shelf above and struck the burner alight by hand. The striker scraped twice before it caught.

Physical action as emotional data. The striker scraping twice before it catches tells us the tool is unfamiliar, which tells us she hasn't been using it, which tells us she's been channeling. The spilled water on the saucer and the absence of any comment from either character add two more layers without a word of explanation.

**2. Lines 253-275 (the "I made jokes for twenty-three years" confession)**

> "I performed the outside of me so well I forgot there was an inside. I would catch myself, you know, walking home, alone, on a stairwell, and I would notice my face. I would notice my face was set in the small smile I used at dinner. Walking alone. On a stairwell. Nobody to see it. The smile was just there. Like a coat I'd forgotten to take off."

The involuntary smile on an empty stairwell is specific, embarrassing, and terrifying in its implication that the performer has fused with the performance. Comma-restart stammer deployed precisely for self-interruption: "there was no, there was no Ash behind the Ash."

**3. Lines 539-569 (the salt-pass and shared non-laugh)**

> He watched her pass the salt. There was nothing in the passing. Her hand went out, the salt went across, his father took it. There was no half-beat in her. The half-beat that had been there for twenty-three years... was not in the gesture. [...] Maren did not laugh, because Maren did not laugh at her own jokes, and Ash did not laugh either, but he caught his sister's eye across the table for the second of a second, and she caught his, and that was the laugh.

The absence of the half-beat is more legible than its presence would have been, because it has never been named before -- its naming via its absence is the chapter's most controlled move. The shared non-laugh as the truest laugh seals it.

### Hearthkeeper

**1. Lines 325-339 (the "I'm Davan" recognition)**

> "In the way of the fight. In the way of the. The proving. The doing-the-fight at the size of the doing-the-fight, because the fight is the thing you have, because if the fight isn't the thing then what was. What was all of it for." [...] "I built my life on a pool," she said. "On a draw. On the precise pulse."

The thematic live wire: Maren recognizing herself in Davan. The only draft to make the parallel explicit. Fragment stacking suits the character's register at this moment.

**2. Lines 306-315 (the efficiency technique callback)**

> He told her about Tesha at the bench. He told her about Liesel from the lower quarter [...] He told her how he had stood at the upper terrace afterwards and had thought, the man had built the ground for the thing he had not, at the end, built.

The only draft to weave Ch19 material into the Ch20 conversation. Makes Ash's emotional state at arrival legible without requiring a cold open. "It felt like watching someone lose a thing he had earned the right to be the one to build" is precise.

**3. Lines 717-732 (the kitchen-felt-different close)**

> The kitchen, in the count, had the shape it had not, in the kitchen's life, had. [...] His mother turned the meat. His father picked up the bread. His sister cut carrots. Ash drank his tea. The cup, in his hands, was warm.

Four short declaratives delivering the "nothing external changes" beat the outline demands. The cup-warmth image callback works.

### Lyricist

**1. Lines 135-138 (the "silence with furniture in it")**

> It was not the silence of two people who had nothing to say. It was the other silence, the silence with the furniture in it, the silence of a room in which a thing too large to walk around had been sitting between two people for so long that they had, both of them, learned the small habits of walking around it, the small turn at the hip when you came past the thing, the small tilt of the shoulder that kept you from grazing it...

The extended metaphor earns its length because it physically maps the thirty-year choreography the chapter will dismantle. Controlled lyricism used correctly.

**2. Lines 313-316 (the word "luck")**

> The word *luck* sat in the kitchen. He had heard the word in his life. He had said the word in his life. [...] he had not, in the hearing, ever heard it said in the voice his sister had just said it in, which was the voice of a person who had been carrying the word in her mouth for twenty-three years and had not, in the carrying, ever set it down.

"Luck" is the right word: it names the cosmic arbitrariness neither sibling can address. The distinction between Ash saying it carefully and Maren saying it as release clarifies the guilt confession without explaining it.

**3. Lines 557-561 (the grief passage)**

> The grief was for the boy in the doorway with the joke ready and the rent due, and the girl at the small lamp at the desk before the rest of the house was up, channeling the light with the guilt at the back of the channeling, and the two of them, at seven and at five, dividing themselves into the complementary shapes the family they loved had needed them to be...

"The house had stood." Three words doing enormous work. The "accidental" and "honest" quality of the children's sacrifice lands in exactly the register the outline requires.

## Weakest Passages

### Carver

**1. Lines 217-219 (the "He had rehearsed sentences" paragraph)**
"Sentences that absolved her. Sentences that made it small. Sentences that turned the moment..." Names the emotional category rather than finding a physical or behavioral detail. Replace with a single physical beat.

**2. Lines 487-489 ("She did not ask him to")**
After "He did not make the joke." -- narrator grading his restraint. Per the No Trailing-Reframe Rule, cut. "He did not make the joke" is sufficient.

**3. Lines 617-643 (closing stairwell paragraph)**
"He noticed not catching it" is slightly over-written. Draws attention to the notation rather than the absence. "He kept walking" is the chapter's correct last word. Cut the middle sentence.

### Hearthkeeper

**1. Lines 295-310 ("the conversation did not, after that, run")**
Exposition telling us what the conversation felt like rather than dramatizing it. The surges-and-stops structure is adequately shown by the following dialogue. The meta-commentary is redundant.

**2. Lines 363-380 (Ash confession: excessive hedging)**
"I have loved you and there is not a count in my life I have not. I have not. Loved you." The stammer multiplies past usefulness. Compare Carver's productive incompletion ("Not you. The fact. The cosmic, at the. Whatever divided us at the beginning.").

**3. Lines 399-406 (Maren's immediate self-referential sympathy)**
"I would have hated me." / "thank you" two lines later -- receipt of the confession as a gift rather than a blow. Lets both characters off the hook simultaneously, foreclosing the devastation the outline requires.

### Lyricist

**1. Lines 189-278 (pool-decline exposition, ~800 words)**
Spring/summer/autumn recitation of Maren's decline in her own voice. Information already shown across Chs 6, 11, and 18. The outline requires decline established through behavior Ash observes, not through a detailed audit she narrates.

**2. Line 275 ("I have been behaving as if I were you")**
Maren stating the sibling mirror-image as a direct equation forecloses the discovery rather than enacting it. The reader and Ash should arrive at the recognition together; she hands them the thesis sentence.

**3. Lines 761-818 (extended walk-home coda)**
The citywide inventory of what is "somewhere in" the city reads as authorially managed rather than organically experienced. The outline closes Ch20 with the kitchen. The Haran workshop coda is surplus.

## Recommended Path

### Primary recommendation: Carver as foundation

The most disciplined draft on every criterion. Only draft that executes all required verbatim exchanges cleanly, handles the crying without melodrama, lands the dinner scene with the salt-pass as change-detector, and closes correctly at the apartment door. Carver's structural rigor: all outline beats in order, no trailing reframes at failure beats, the "X was the X" mannerism used exactly once.

### Passages to pull from other drafts

**From Lyricist, lines 135-138 ("silence with furniture in it")**
Insert in Carver's second scene break, after the tea is poured and before "She was thinner than the last time he had seen her." Characterizes the silence that has been maintained for twenty-three years.

**From Lyricist, line 313 ("The word luck sat in the kitchen")**
The beat between Maren's guilt confession and Ash's "I know. I always knew." passes too quickly in Carver. Insert the single line "The word *luck* sat in the kitchen." as a scene break before Ash answers. If the full passage is too much, a paragraph break alone works; the single line is better.

**From Hearthkeeper, lines 325-339 (Maren-as-Davan recognition, compressed)**
The chapter's thematic connection to the book's arc is missing without this parallel. The causal hook in the outline is explicit: watching Davan crack in Ch19 is what sends Ash to the apartment. Insert a brief Davan reference after Ash's cheerfulness-cost speech. Maren's recognition of herself does not require "I'm Davan" verbatim -- it can land in her gesture, her quarter-turn of the cup, her silence. But the parallel must be present.

12. Dinner and salt-pass: Carver as-is.

## Revision Notes

**1. (Mandatory) Cut the "He had rehearsed sentences" paragraph (Carver lines 217-219)**
Replace with a single physical beat. The named-emotion logic ("Sentences that absolved her") fails show-don't-tell. One physical detail carries more weight than the three-sentence inventory.

**2. (Mandatory) Cut "She did not ask him to" (Carver lines 487-489)**
No Trailing-Reframe Rule. "He did not make the joke" closes the beat. The following sentence is narrator grading his restraint. Remove.

**3. (Mandatory) Insert Davan-Maren parallel (from Hearthkeeper)**
The thematic connection to the book's arc requires some version of this. The causal hook from Ch19 is explicit in the outline. Insert after Ash's cheerfulness-cost speech. Can be rendered through Maren's recognition without stating it.

**4. (Important) Cut "He noticed not catching it" from the closing passage**
"He kept walking" is the chapter's correct last word. The middle sentence deflates the control.

**5. (Important) Insert Lyricist's "luck" isolation (one line or paragraph break)**
The beat between the guilt confession and "I know. I always knew." needs space. Minimum: a paragraph break. Better: "The word *luck* sat in the kitchen."

**6. (Important) Confirm Joren continuity**
Both Hearthkeeper and Lyricist name Joren as Maren's superior. Outline does not establish this name. If used in the final draft, add to Ch20 outline character list and reconcile against Chs 6, 11, and 18.

**7. (Polish) Verify integration motion status**
Lyricist has the integration motion passing as background news at dinner. Outline leaves it pending at Ch19's close. If carried into the final draft, requires outline update at Ch19 continuity hooks.
