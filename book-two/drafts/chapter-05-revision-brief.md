# Revision Brief — Chapter 5

## Critical Issues (must fix)

**1. Trailing reframes — mandatory cuts (No Trailing-Reframe Rule)**

- The italicized callback `*Don't measure me against you. Measure you against you.*` in the reservoir-math section (line ~321) appears as a consoling echo after an anxiety/failure-adjacent beat. Cut it. The math passage stands without it.
- The final sentence of the chapter ("Behind him the workshop window stayed lit a long while, and then went out, and Stonecurve held its small new warmth in its sealed walls and slept") is an elegiac narrator-side reframe. The true close is "He walked home in the dark." Everything after that sentence is the narrator doing what the chapter's action has already done. Cut.

**2. Ash's identity dread is absent from the reservoir math.**
The chapter gives Ash intellectual anxiety about the scaling arithmetic but never connects it to the emotional core: the fear of returning to who he was before the Wellspring. The reservoir math is a plot problem as written; it needs to also be a character problem. The connection does not need to be named but must be visible in what Ash does or avoids doing.

**3. Maren's seed is absent, and Chapter 6 depends on it.**
The plan designates a Ch5 texture-plant for Maren's pool weakening — not a scene, but enough signal that Ch6's micro-hesitation reads as confirmation rather than new information. Options: Ash noting she hasn't come by, a brief reference to a channeler colleague showing fatigue, or a passing observation Ash files but doesn't name. Without it the Ch6 payoff is weakened.

**4. The Levelers are invisible through five chapters of Act One.**
The series outline requires Leveler seeds throughout Act One before the Act Two crystallization. Chapter 5 passes entirely inside Stonecurve without any background signal of political organizing. A single line from Felden about broadsheets near the Hall, or a comment from one of the district workers, would close the gap. At the current rate, Davan's arrival will feel imported.

**5. Haran's two direct-lesson lines violate his voice spec.**
- "That's the right answer." — delivers a verdict like Father, not a Haran resource-reframe. Cut or replace with a pivot back to the work: e.g., "Does Tesha know that yet."
- "You can't train everyone. You train enough. The enough train more." — three declarative thesis sentences in anaphora. Not Haran's mode. Replace with a sideways mechanism analogy: e.g., "Renar's pump still runs. He's been dead twelve years." or simply pivot to a resource question about Block Four.

**6. "She'd like a report." is cross-character collapse (Ash reads as Maren).**
This line is clipped, dry, knowing, corrective, and unhedged — Maren's register, not Ash's. Ash doesn't push back against Dorenne's representatives in words. Replace with compliance plus over-eager pivot: "Right, yes, of course — let me show you the cistern first." (No em dash; use comma.)

## Important Suggestions (should address)

**7. The efficiency-to-reservoir-math amplification is adjacent, not convergent.**
Ash thinks about Haran's whisper in the evening wall-sit and separately thinks about the scaling math, but the two threads never collide. The sentence "He knew it was the only currency in which the math could change" is the hinge but doesn't turn. Add one beat where Ash runs the math against Haran's efficiency rate rather than his own — even imprecisely — so the connection becomes a gut-punch rather than a filed observation.

**8. Narrator-side emotional gloss on Tesha's face (line ~295) — cut.**
"Her face settled, slowly, into something neither proud nor ashamed but simpler: she had done it. The pump worked. The water came." This names the emotional state after the physical detail (hand lingering on the flue) has already communicated it. Cut the face-settling sentence and the three-sentence gloss. End on her picking up the tool roll and walking.

**9. "He didn't say what comes after the work. He didn't have to." — cut.**
The Felden scene has already established this. The narrator confirming what the reader understood shrinks the beat. Cut. End on "He walked. He did not look back again."

**10. The dependency trap needs a sharper hairline fracture at the close.**
The final wall-sit enumerates what's working and ends on "A start" and "Yet." Per the outline, this chapter should hold hope and hairline fracture together. Currently the weight tips toward earned satisfaction. Ash's enumeration should include something that isn't working yet, or someone who still can't, filed without resolution. Reversing the order so "Yet" comes before "nobody was channeling, and nobody needed to" would shift the weight from comfort to unease.

**11. "A whisper. Where Ash would have used a shout." — trim.**
The scene already dramatizes this perfectly. The two-sentence gloss names the implication for a reader who has already arrived. Cut "Where Ash would have used a shout" or both sentences. The paragraph ends more powerfully on "dropped into Haran's palm."

**12. Vell's competence arc is entirely told — give her one failure scene.**
Everything after her introduction is the reader watching someone who has already become skilled. A single half-page scene of Vell making the priming mistake (day one or two, Ash watching, she tries again without asking for help) would earn the shoulder-softening and the Haran exchange. The Tesha flue sequence works because the reader watches failure and re-attempt in real time; Vell deserves the same.

**13. Dael's arc beat for this chapter is missing.**
The outline designates Ch5 as the chapter where Dael shifts toward heating-crew ownership. He appears by name once (line ~229) in a walk-past list. Either add a moment of Dael's shift in the heating section (a line, a choice he makes that shows ownership forming) or update the outline to reflect the beat has moved.

**14. The reservoir cost of the second week needs a physical moment.**
"Forty-one percent at the start of the second week...closer to thirty-nine" is delivered as a ledger entry. The chapter is otherwise careful to make reservoir cost physical. At the end of the flue-sealing sequence, two sentences where the week's draw registers in the body — not crisis, just honest accumulation — would keep the tracking in the body rather than on a spreadsheet.

**15. Give Ash's Felden-scene dialogue more chatter-armor.**
Per the voice spec, Ash under evaluation from Dorenne's representative should talk more, not less. His near-silence through the tour (two- and three-word fragments) compresses him into Dorenne's register. He would have talked through the whole tour, guiding the narrative, flagging things before they could be questioned, making small jokes he'd walk back. A few additional lines in the tour sequence would show the drift-into-compression as something that has happened to him, not as who he has become.

The adverb "quietly" in "said, quietly, 'She's faster than Corren'" is doing work the framing already handles. Cut the adverb: "He walked back to where Haran was repacking a tool roll. 'She's faster than Corren.'"

No attribution issue — this is clear. But "He didn't have an answer" immediately following is a mild narrator-side explanation of what the silence already said. Consider cutting.

Haran references "the Velden pump" as a cost comparison without establishment in the gazetteer or any prior chapter. Either add it to the Named Locations gazetteer (type: prior project reference, established Ch5) or confirm it appears in Book One.

**22. No "So." opener anywhere in a Haran-heavy chapter.**

**23. Felden's "small repertoire of acknowledgments" list is slightly expository.**

**24. Dimming-city motif ("going dark a window at a time") appears twice in this chapter.**
