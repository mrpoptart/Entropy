## Best Passages

### Carver

**1. Haran's cellar diagnosis (lines 43-67)**

> "Thirty seams in this riser. One of them's gone. We'll find it."
> ...
> "Second riser. Two seams above the manifold. The flux didn't take."
> "Last week."
> "Last week. The day Vell was sick and we ran two short. I did that joint. I did it tired."
> He said it without weight. He said it the way he said most things he meant.

Best Haran passage in any of the three drafts. Failure owned without drama. The distinction between weight and meaning in "He said it without weight. He said it the way he said most things he meant" establishes a character's entire relationship with accountability.

**2. The broadsheet's critical line (line 235)**

> *One is tempted, in the presence of such ingenuity, to ask whether the Wellspring's workshop is the future of civic infrastructure or a charming exercise in restraint, undertaken by the most powerful channeler of the age in the spirit of a wealthy man who has chosen, for a season, to walk to work.*

The chapter's single most devastating sentence. The mannered essayist register enacts the condescension it describes. "For a season" frames Ash's project as leisure.

**3. Jora's teaching scene (lines 753-799, before the trailing-reframe violation)**

> "Don't skip the count," Jora said. "The count is for looking. The count is when you find the buckle before it's a buckle."
> ...
> "This," Jora said, "is the priming valve. It does what the Wellspring says priming valves do."

Dependency seed delivered as overheard teaching. "The Wellspring says" without irony is efficient thematic delivery.

### Hearthkeeper

**1. Pell's arrival scene (lines 171-309)**

> "I came about the heating. Vell decided I was about the pump."
> ...
> "That doesn't carry," he said. "To us. We don't have your reservoir. We can't reknit the plaster. So what I am taking back, when I take this design back, is a wall I cannot build."

Hearthkeeper introduces the dependency problem from an unexpected angle: the rural outsider naming it plainly before Ash has thought it through. "A wall I cannot build" is the cleanest statement of the transferability problem in any draft.

**2. The letters' physical contrast (lines 453-453)**

> The two letters lay there, the cheap paper and the cream paper, the fast hand and the deliberate hand, the question without an answer and the answer without a question, and the bench beneath them was scarred from forty years of Haran's tools and the lamp above them burned the same flame it burned every afternoon at this hour.

Each term advances rather than restates: cheap/cream, fast/deliberate, question-without-answer/answer-without-question. The bench's forty years is the right counter-weight.

**3. The square speaker's opening argument (lines 553-577)**

> "...and the maintenance is what they don't talk about. They talk about the construction. The construction is the easy part. The construction is one expenditure, one spend, one push, and then the pictures get drawn for the broadsheets and the broadsheets go up and everybody nods. The maintenance is what asks you, every week, every month, whether you meant it."

The speaker builds from specific upward (maintenance before redistribution). "Whether you meant it" lands directly on Ash's actual vulnerability: not his politics but his commitment.

### Lyricist

**1. The residents naming their systems (lines 160-169)**

> The pump in the east square was *Vell's pump*... The system in Building Four, the failing one, was *the lazy one* before the failure and *the bastard* after, and the residents said *the bastard* with the affection a man saves for a tool that has, in the end, mostly worked.
> Ash stood in the east square at fifth-watch on a clear day and listened to a woman say *Granny* to her daughter and felt, for the second it took the word to land, that the work he had done had become something he did not own.

"The bastard" is the strongest of any version's names. Ash's feeling that "the work...had become something he did not own" is the correct ambivalent register for the scene.

**2. The Bess/Lin teaching scene (lines 656-739)**

> "The pause is for looking. Don't skip the pause. The pause is when you find the buckle before it's a buckle."
> ...
> He saw the teaching.
> He did not see the lineage.

Only draft that fully executes the revision brief's dependency seed requirement. "He did not see the lineage" is the flattest, most correct delivery of what the brief mandated.

**3. Beddan's arrival and the twenty-two-village revelation (lines 343-493)**

> "We've been late, Ash."
> "We've been late."
> He picked up the file. He did not, for some minutes, move it.

"He did not, for some minutes, move it" is the best Haran physical-beat business in any draft. Correct weight for a man receiving confirmation the world's emergency is older and larger than he thought.

## Weakest Passages

### Carver

**1. The trailing-reframe coda (lines 899-903)**
All three sentences ("The reservoir sat...the small ordinary night of a place that worked," "The shelf...was, by morning, fuller than it had ever been," "He did not, by morning, lift the cloth.") are mandatory cuts under the No Trailing-Reframe Rule. End at "He slept."

**2. Haran's letter-scene speech (lines 610-612)**
"You're building a thing the powerful people don't need and the powerless people can't afford to wait for. Both letters are correct. Both letters are about themselves." Three declarative sentences in Dorenne's register. Replace with a mechanism analogy.

**3. The cultural-convergence narrator gloss (line 209)**
"She said it the way she said most things, which was the way Tesha said most things, which was the way Haran said most things, which was, Ash registered without naming, beginning to be the way the district said most things." Announces cultural convergence as narrator conclusion. Cut entirely.

### Hearthkeeper

**1. The Dorenne-scene paragraph (lines 531-536)**
"He went up to Dorenne's office in the afternoon and was back before the fifteenth hour, and the conversation he had with her did not need to be recounted..." Structural evasion followed by narrator metaphor for the relationship. Cut the second clause.

**2. Reska's departure scene continuity issue (lines 87-143)**
Reska appears as morning-crew chief in Carver's chapter (lines 157-215). Naming her as a quitter here creates continuity drift. The quitter names should be Berrin and Marda (Hearthkeeper) or Aren and the unnamed boy (Carver). Fix naming.

**3. Ash's internal processing after the square (lines 631-641)**
"the sentence he had been writing in his own head in different words for three weeks" is analysis, not sensation. The phrase-recognition beat belongs in the square scene, not retrospect.

### Lyricist

**1. The POV break at chapter close (lines 737-740, 779-781)**
"The reader, in the square in the doorway, saw both." / "Ash did not see the knot. / The reader did." Violates strict third-limited POV and undermines the effect by announcing it. Cut and replace with free indirect rendering.

**2. Korvin Delth's departure observation (lines 339-340)**
"Had not looked back from a thing in his life since he was twelve" -- Ash speculating about a man's entire character from body posture after twenty minutes. Unearned analytical confidence.

**3. "It was enough, he told himself. Enough for the day." (line 565)**
Trailing reframe on a failure-adjacent beat. End on "He pushed the drawer until it closed enough."

## Recommended Path

### Primary recommendation: Carver as foundation

Carver is the strongest draft across every criterion except revision brief adherence. Its violations are specific cuts and targeted repairs rather than structural problems. Its pacing architecture is the cleanest.

### Passages to pull from other drafts

**From Hearthkeeper, the Pell rural liaison scene (lines 171-309)**
Replace Carver's Othen scene (lines 399-543) with an adaptation of Hearthkeeper's Pell scene. Preserve "that doesn't carry" and "a wall I cannot build" verbatim. Insert after the broadsheet scene, before Velden.

**From Hearthkeeper, the "That's an Ash sentence" dependency beat (lines 663-720)**
Replace Carver's Jora teaching beat at lines 789-796 with an adaptation of this exchange. The deflection of "why three-quarters?" to "I'll ask Ash" sharpens the dependency seed precisely.

**From Lyricist, the residents' naming catalogue (lines 157-169)**
Add a 250-300 word observation scene (in Carver's register) before the broadsheet section. Preserve "the bastard" as the name for Building Four's system. Cut "He was not sure, in the second after, whether the feeling was a good one" -- render ambivalence physically.

**From Lyricist, "He saw the teaching. / He did not see the lineage." (after line 735)**
Insert into Carver's Jora scene after "He thought, this is what I'm building. / He thought, this is people teaching people." (lines 813-815). Do not add "The reader saw both."

### Hybrid structure

1. Carver scenes 1-4 (cold-call through broadsheet), targeted surgery per revision notes.
2. Hearthkeeper's Pell scene (adapted to Carver's prose register), replacing Carver's Othen scene.
3. Carver's Velden scene with one hedge per brief Issue 9.
4. Lyricist's residents-naming passage (short, 250-300 words), inserted before broadsheet.
5. Carver's letters scene, Haran's lecture replaced by mechanism analogy.
6. Carver's Halver's Square scene: phrase-recognition moved into moment of impact; "Not today" framed as physiological failure not composed restraint.
7. Carver's Jora/shift-change scene with Hearthkeeper's "That's an Ash sentence" and Lyricist's "He did not see the lineage."
8. End at "He slept." All trailing-reframe coda cut.

## Revision Notes

**1. (Mandatory) Cut the three-sentence coda after "He slept." (lines 899-903)**
No Trailing-Reframe Rule, 100% cut. End at "He slept." No replacement.

**2. (Mandatory) Replace Haran's letter-scene lecture with mechanism analogy (lines 610-612)**
"Both letters are correct. Both letters are about themselves." -- Dorenne's register. Replace with analogy: "Joint gets stress from two directions at once. Doesn't mean either direction is wrong." Return the question to Ash.

**3. (Mandatory) Cut the cultural-convergence gloss (line 209)**
"beginning to be the way the district said most things" -- narrator announcing the blind spot that Ash should have. The pump-naming detail already does this work.

**4. (Mandatory) Add dependency-seed shadow-beat to the Jora teaching scene**
One concrete beat of something-slightly-wrong when the recruit writes "the Wellspring says" onto her slate a second time, or Jora deflecting a technical question to Ash. Without this, "He did not see the lineage" is unearned.

**5. (Important) Move the phrase-recognition beat into the Halver's Square scene**
Currently placed in the walk-home retrospect (lines 629-633). Must be rendered physically at the moment the speaker says the phrase. "Not today" should arrive as physiological failure, not composed restraint.
